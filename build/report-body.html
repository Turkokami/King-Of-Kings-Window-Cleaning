01

The verdict

King of Kings has the largest site in its market — 287 URLs against Mt. Baker's 213 — the second-largest review base at 248 Google reviews, and copy that does real local research: the Ferndale page talks about Cherry Point fallout, farm dust and hard-water spotting. It still scores fourth of seven.

The reason is narrow and fixable. Those 287 pages are attached to no business entity. There is not one LocalBusiness, Organization or Service node anywhere on the domain, and they sit in a flat URL space — 275 of 287 URLs are root-level slugs with no /services/ or /locations/ directory — so nothing gives that authority a shape to travel through.

The rebuild is not a rescue. It is a rehousing: take a complete 150-page city × service matrix and a genuine 248-review reputation, put them on a real entity graph and a real taxonomy, take every page to the depth floor, and extend the geo lattice from 25 markets to 41 and from 12 neighborhood pages to 62.

02

How this was measured

Every URL in the four live sitemaps was fetched anonymously — credentials:'omit', cache:'no-store' — at concurrency 6, and parsed for the Part 9.4 field set: title and length, meta description and length, canonical, robots, H1 count and text, full H2 list, body word count, image and missing-alt counts, every JSON-LD node type and @id, and every internal outbound link. All 287 returned 200. Nothing here is estimated.

The cloud sandbox's egress policy blocks this host, so the sweep ran from the operator's own browser — the same live-site-is-the-source-of-truth method Keystone requires, and the same one that produced the King of Kings lessons folded into this standard. Lab performance was sampled logged-out over the WP Rocket cache. Two of the six competitors could not be navigated to under the browser extension's site permissions and were measured through a server-side fetch instead; their rows are marked as medium confidence.

04

What is already right

Worth stating plainly, because it changes the scope. This is not a broken site — most of the failure modes this portfolio sees on inherited Elementor builds are simply absent here, and that is why the rebuild is an upgrade path rather than a teardown.

  • Exactly one H1 on all 287 pages. No missing-H1 epidemic, no doubles. Elementor builds in this portfolio routinely fail this.
  • One JSON-LD emitter per page — no duplicate-node collision of the kind that cost weeks on a previous build.
  • Zero orphans, zero duplicate titles, zero canonical mismatches, zero accidental noindex, zero non-200s, zero schema parse errors.
  • The city × service matrix is complete — 150 of 150, no holes. That is a rare starting asset and it migrates straight across.
  • The local research in the copy is real. Cherry Point industrial fallout, the Nooksack Valley, agricultural dust, hard-water spotting. The rewrite is a depth problem, not a blank page.
  • 248 Google reviews and six live social profiles. The off-page half of the entity already exists; only the on-page half is missing.
  • TTFB 149 ms, load 595 ms. Hosting and cache are not the bottleneck.
  • Nothing fabricated. No invented ratings, no phantom pricing, no false credentials, consistent NAP.
  • Seven real awards, all third-party and dated — including Cascades Best Gold for Best Cleaning Service in 2025 and 2026 from The Bellingham Herald, and CommunityVotes Platinum for Best Window Cleaning. None of it is in schema, and it is sitting one page away from the "Bellingham's Best" claim it could substitute for.
  • A named owner who is actually on the job. Randy Fee founded the company in March 2024, personally trains every technician, and is named by customers in their own reviews. Hundreds of reviews and seven awards in under three years is a better story than "decades of experience" — and it is unused.

What to take

  • Mt. Baker's geo trio. GeoCoordinates + PostalAddress + Place hung off the LocalBusiness node. Free, and King of Kings has none of it.
  • Mt. Baker's hub behaviour. 107 internal links off their homepage. King of Kings' homepage absorbs 292 inbound links and passes 15 out.
  • ProWash's hub depth. 3,601 words on the homepage — depth on the hub, not just the spokes.
  • Whatcom GRC's service split. They sell moss prevention separately from moss removal, and gutter installation and gutter guards as their own services. Three commercial intents King of Kings currently gives away.

What to leave

  • ProWash's 104-character title, and an H1 reading "Gutter cleaning and Christmas light installer" on a page whose title sells pressure washing.
  • NW Window Cleaning's sixteen-city meta description standing in for city pages.
  • Pure Shine's 94-image homepage.
  • The whole market's unqualified guarantee language. Nobody here publishes terms — that is an opening to take, not a licence to copy.

The taxonomy

One taxonomy per domain, decided before Phase 1 and never mixed. Every internal-link rewrite is domain-guarded — matched as kingofkingswindowcleaning.com/services/…, never the bare string — and every redirect points at its final destination.

The current site has no taxonomy to migrate from — 275 of 287 URLs are flat root-level slugs, and the only directories in use are /case-studies/ (5 pages) and /category/ (6). 197 page-by-page 301s are mapped, including 26 that collapse cannibalising duplicates into a single canonical page.

Open items before Phase 1

  • Named expert — resolved. Randy Fee, owner. Wired into the Person node, the expert block on every page and his own entity page at /team/randy-fee/. Still owed: his bio, a real photo, and his years in the trade before founding the company in March 2024.
  • The word "Licensed". The site says licensed and L&I insured, but the only identifier findable anywhere is the UBI — which is a business licence, not a contractor registration. Window cleaning generally doesn't require one in Washington; roof cleaning and pressure washing can. Randy should confirm with L&I. Until then hasCredential stays out and the credential line reads "Washington business licence and L&I coverage", which is true.
  • UBI publication. 605476674 is already visible on the live site. Confirm in writing that it stays published in schema — Part 8 makes publishing an identifier a client decision.
  • The filed address. The Secretary of State filing carries a residential principal office address. It is public record and it is staying out of the build — schema carries locality and region only, matching the legacy site's own correct choice. Flagging it so the decision is explicit rather than accidental.
  • Guarantee terms. "Risk-free" runs on 74 pages with no terms. The actual terms need writing before /our-guarantee/ can exist, and every guarantee string links there.
  • Review count. 248 is the on-page claim. aggregateRating gets pulled from the verified GBP at build time and never hand-entered.
  • Service confirmations. Does he sell gutter installation and gutter guards? Moss prevention as a standalone job? Christmas lights? Build only the confirmed set — a vertical page for work he doesn't do is a liability.
  • Oak Harbor. It is Island County, outside the chosen Whatcom + Skagit territory, and it is live today with six pages. Retain and maintain, or let it go?
  • Sixteen new markets. Point Roberts, Lummi Island, Guemes Island and Samish Island all need a truthful travel-and-scheduling answer before their pages can claim service.
