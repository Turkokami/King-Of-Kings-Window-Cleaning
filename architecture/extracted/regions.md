## Four regions, four different kinds of dirty

Whatcom and Skagit are not one environment, and glass in one part of the service
area gets dirty in a way glass ten miles away does not. This is the whole reason
the town pages exist rather than one page with a list of place names on it.

**The marine edge** — Blaine, Semiahmoo, Birch Bay, Anacortes, Guemes and Samish
Island, Point Roberts. Salt. It arrives as aerosol on the wind, coats windward
glass in an even haze, and is hygroscopic, meaning it pulls moisture out of the
air and stays damp long after everything else has dried. That damp film picks up
everything else that blows past. Waterfront glass on the west and south
elevations often needs three visits a year where a sheltered house inland needs
one.

**The industrial corridors** — the Cherry Point area west of Ferndale and Custer,
and the refinery ground near Anacortes. Fine particulate, evenly distributed,
persistent, and almost never visible as a mark you can point to. It reads as
glass that has gone slightly grey and never looks properly clean, most obvious
when low winter sun comes through at an angle.

**The agricultural valleys** — the Nooksack Valley through Lynden, Everson,
Nooksack and Sumas, and the Skagit flats around Mount Vernon, Burlington, Conway
and Bow. Fine soil dust in quantity, concentrated into the dry weeks of late
summer, and it combines with overnight moisture into a film that dries on rather
than rinsing off. The seasonal pattern here is the most predictable thing in the
whole service area.

**The wet foothills** — Deming, Acme, Van Zandt, Maple Falls, Glacier, Kendall,
and the shaded lakeside ground at Sudden Valley and Geneva. Rainfall, deep
conifer cover, and the shade that comes with it. Less airborne dirt, far more
organic growth: green film in the frame corners, algae on north elevations, and
sap and needle debris on anything under a tree.

Most properties sit in one of these. Some sit in two — Ferndale gets industrial
haze *and* valley dust, which is why its glass has a reputation for never looking
right for long.
