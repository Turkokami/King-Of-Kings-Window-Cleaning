## Siding material by siding material

**Vinyl** is the most common siding in both counties on anything built since the
1970s, and it is the most forgiving to soft wash. Two cautions. Old vinyl goes
brittle from UV and cracks under force. And vinyl is where oxidation shows most —
know the difference between a wall that is dirty and a wall that is faded, because
only one of them washes clean.

**Fiber cement** — HardiePlank and similar, standard on newer construction here.
Durable and washes well. The vulnerable parts are the cut ends and any place the
factory coating has been compromised, where water gets into the board itself. Low
pressure only; fiber cement will erode under a jet.

**Cedar shingle and cedar lap** — common on older Bellingham housing and around
Chuckanut, Edgemoor and the lake. Absorbent, so saturation has to be controlled;
gentle solution, and realistic expectations about weathered cedar. Silvered cedar
cleans up but does not return to fresh cedar colour.

**Painted wood** — older housing throughout both counties. Sound paint washes
fine. Failing paint comes off, and that is a conversation to have at the quote.

**Engineered wood (LP SmartSide and similar)** — increasingly common on newer
builds. Cleans well, but the substrate is wood-based, so anywhere the coating is
breached is a place water should not be driven.

**Stucco and EIFS** — a smaller share of the housing stock here. Porous, and
cracks are common in older applications. Low pressure, and cracking gets flagged,
because water behind stucco is a far more serious problem than growth on it.

**Brick** — durable, but older lime mortar joints erode under pressure. Cleaned
gently.
