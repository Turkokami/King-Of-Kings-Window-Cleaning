## The post-construction warning

Post-build glass is the one job where the wrong approach causes permanent,
expensive damage, so it is worth being explicit.

Tempered glass — used in and around doors, in low openings, in large panes, and
increasingly everywhere in new construction — carries microscopic surface debris
fused into the pane during the tempering process. It is latent. It is invisible.
And aggressive scraping across it drags that debris and produces fine scratching
across the whole pane. The scratches were caused by the blade, but the
vulnerability was in the glass from the factory, which is exactly why the
resulting arguments about who pays are so unpleasant.

This is a well-known phenomenon in the trade and it is the reason a builder's
clean is not the same product as a maintenance clean, and is not priced like one.
If a new build has debris on the glass, get someone who knows what is under the
blade.
