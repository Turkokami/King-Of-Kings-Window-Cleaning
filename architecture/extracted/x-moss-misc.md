## Moss on roofs that are not composition shingle

The mechanism described above is specific to overlapping courses, which means it
plays out differently depending on what your roof is made of.

**Cedar shake and shingle.** The worst case, and common on older Bellingham
housing, on the lake, and around Chuckanut and Edgemoor. Wood is absorbent, so
moss is not merely sitting on the surface — it is growing in a material that
holds moisture itself, and it establishes deep in the checking and splits of
weathered shakes as well as between the courses. Cedar cannot take aggressive
treatment or aggressive removal: shakes split, and the material is already the
thing decaying. Treatment is gentler, slower, and expectations have to be
realistic. There is more on
[moss on cedar shake](/services/roof-cleaning/cedar-shake-moss/).

**Metal.** Standing seam and corrugated roofs do not offer moss a foothold on the
panel itself, but the seams, the fixings and the low-slope transitions collect
debris and moss establishes in that. The concern is the factory coating: the
wrong chemistry dulls or strips it, and the coating is what makes the roof last.
See [metal roof soft washing](/services/metal-roof-soft-washing/).

**Concrete and clay tile.** Tile is porous and moss establishes readily in the
surface and in the laps. The tile itself is not really threatened by it, but the
weight of a heavily mossed tile roof and the moisture retention against the
battens and underlayment are the real considerations. The bigger risk on tile is
foot traffic during the work — tile breaks underfoot far more readily than
asphalt does.

**Torch-down and low-slope membrane.** Growth here is usually algae and standing
debris rather than moss proper, and the vulnerable points are the seams. Cleaning
is gentle and the useful part of the visit is often clearing the drains.

## The false economies

Three things homeowners commonly do that cost more than they save.

**Waiting until it looks bad.** Moss is doing the lifting described above long
before it is visible from the street, and it does most of that work in the wet
half of the year when nobody is looking at the roof. By the time a roof looks
mossy from the driveway, the growth under the courses is well established.

**Buying the cheapest quote.** The cheapest roof-moss quote in this market is
frequently a pressure wash, because pressure washing is fast. It will look
excellent for a season and it will have taken years off the roof, and the damage
is not reversible.

**Doing it yourself off a ladder.** Wet moss on a pitched roof in February is one
of the slicker working surfaces in residential trades, and the injury statistics
for homeowner roof work are what they are. The moss most worth removing is at the
eaves and in the valleys, which is where the footing is worst.

**Treating and then never clearing the gutters.** Everything killed on the roof
comes down over the following two months. A treatment followed by no gutter
clearing produces a blocked system going into winter, which is how a solved
problem creates a new one.
