## Decks deserve their own conversation

More decks are damaged by pressure washing than any other surface, so it is worth
separating deck cleaning from the rest of this page.

A softwood deck — cedar, pressure-treated fir, hemlock — is soft, fibrous and
directional. Pressure applied across it rather than along the grain, or held too
close, tears the surface fibres and raises them. The board then feels rough
underfoot, holds dirt in the raised grain, sheds water less well, and takes stain
in a blotchy way that cannot be corrected without sanding. The damage is often
not obvious on the wet day of the clean; it appears as the boards dry.

The correct approach on wood is chemical rather than mechanical: a cleaning
solution appropriate to the wood, dwell time, and a gentle rinse at pressure low
enough that the wand is essentially just moving water. It looks unimpressive
while it is happening and the result is far better a week later.

Composite decking is a different question again. It does not rot, but the
polymer and wood-flour surface has a manufacturer-specified maximum pressure, and
exceeding it both damages the surface and voids the warranty. Some manufacturers
specify no pressure washing at all. We work to whatever the manufacturer says
rather than to what the machine can do.

If a deck needs stripping and refinishing rather than cleaning, that is a
different trade and we will say so. We clean decks. We do not sand, strip, stain
or seal them.
