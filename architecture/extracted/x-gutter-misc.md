## Gutter materials, and what each one needs

**Seamless aluminium** is what is on most houses built here in the last forty
years. Light, rust-proof, and soft — which matters, because it dents and it
scratches, and a spike driven back into a soft fascia will not hold for long.
Aluminium is also the material that shows tiger striping most visibly, because
the painted finish oxidises and gives the runoff residue something to bond to.

**Galvanised steel** turns up on older houses. Stronger than aluminium and
considerably heavier, and it rusts once the coating is compromised — usually
from the inside, under a permanent layer of wet debris, which is exactly why
letting a steel gutter sit loaded is worse than letting an aluminium one sit
loaded.

**Copper** appears on a small number of higher-end and heritage properties around
Fairhaven, Edgemoor and parts of Anacortes. It is extremely durable and it
patinas rather than corroding, but it is unforgiving of harsh cleaning — the wrong
chemical takes the patina off unevenly and the result is worse than what you
started with. Copper gets cleaned conservatively and we will tell you what we are
not going to attempt.

**Vinyl** shows up on additions, outbuildings and budget installs. It becomes
brittle with UV exposure and cold, and old vinyl sections crack if handled with
any force at all. On vinyl the honest advice is often that a section needs
replacing rather than cleaning.

There is more on each in the [surface library](/surface-library/).

## Commercial and multi-unit gutters

Apartment blocks, HOA-managed properties, storefronts, warehouses and
institutional buildings, on a scheduled contract rather than an as-needed call.

Two things differ from residential work. The first is that the consequence of
failure is bigger and lands on someone else — an overflow at a commercial
entrance is a slip hazard and a liability question, not just a maintenance
annoyance. The second is that commercial buildings frequently have internal
drains, parapet scuppers and flat-roof drainage rather than conventional hung
gutters, which is a different inspection and a different clearing method.

Scheduled contracts get the work booked in before the wet season rather than
after the first complaint, which is the entire value of scheduling it.
