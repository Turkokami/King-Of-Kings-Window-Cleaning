## What to do between treatments

Once a roof has been treated, a few habits genuinely extend the interval before
it needs doing again.

**Look at the north slope in November, not August.** Moss is dormant, shrunken
and grey in dry weather and near-invisible from the ground. It is green, swollen
and obvious in the wet months. Judging your roof in late summer is judging it at
the moment it lies to you most convincingly.

**Watch the gutters as a proxy.** A gutter filling faster than it used to is
usually telling you the roof above it is shedding more organic material than it
used to, and that is often moss debris before the moss itself is conspicuous.

**Keep an eye on the valleys.** They collect first and worst, and they are where
lifted courses do the most damage, because a valley concentrates the water from
two roof planes.

**Do not go up there with a stiff brush in spring.** The urge to knock the dead
material off rather than wait for the weather is understandable and it undoes the
whole point of treating rather than scraping.

**Book the follow-up on a cycle rather than on appearance.** By the time a roof
looks like it needs doing, the growth under the courses has had a couple of
seasons to work. Three to five years, set by shade and elevation rather than by
how it looks from the driveway, is the sensible rhythm.

## Moss elsewhere on the property

Roofs are where moss does structural harm, but it is rarely confined to them.

**In the gutters.** Moss establishes in the silt at the bottom of a gutter and
holds water like a sponge, which is why a mossy gutter can overflow while looking
clear from below. Handled as part of
[gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning/).

**On siding**, particularly on shaded north elevations and on the lower courses
where splash keeps things damp. Handled with
[house washing](/services/house-washing/).

**On walkways, steps, patios and driveways.** This one is a safety matter rather
than an appearance one — wet moss on a concrete step is genuinely slippery, and
in a region with this many wet months that is a fall risk on a path people use
every day. Handled with
[pressure washing](/services/pressure-washing/), where the surface can take it.

**On fences and decks**, where it holds moisture against wood and accelerates
rot.
