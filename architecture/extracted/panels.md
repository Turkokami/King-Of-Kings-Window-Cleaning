## The panel types, and what each one needs

Knowing which roof you have changes the quote and the method, and most people are
not sure which they have.

**Standing seam** is the premium residential system: concealed fasteners, panels
joined by raised vertical seams that are mechanically locked or snapped together.
No screw heads through the panel face, which is why it lasts and why it costs
more. Cleaning attention goes to the seams themselves, which trap debris, and to
the flashings. The seams are also the part most easily damaged by careless foot
traffic — a distorted seam affects how the roof sheds water, which is a repair
rather than a cosmetic issue.

**Exposed-fastener corrugated and ribbed** — the classic agricultural profile,
also common on outbuildings, shops and budget-conscious residential builds.
Screws pass through the panel face into the structure, each with a sealing
washer. Cheaper to install, shorter service life, and the washers are the
maintenance item: they harden, crack and shrink with UV and thermal cycling over
fifteen to twenty years. Cleaning these roofs means working around several
hundred small ledges, and it is the roof type where a look at the fasteners has
the most value.

**Metal shingle and tile profiles**, stamped to imitate slate, shake or tile.
Used on higher-end residential work. More surface detail means more places for
debris to sit and a slower clean.

**Stone-coated steel** is its own case and worth flagging: a steel panel with a
granular stone chip surface bonded to it. It looks like tile and behaves, for
cleaning purposes, more like asphalt shingle than like metal — the granules can
be dislodged by pressure just as shingle granules can. Anyone treating a
stone-coated roof as ordinary metal is likely to damage it.

**Aluminium panels**, common near salt water for obvious reasons. Softer than
steel, dents more easily, does not rust but does corrode galvanically against
dissimilar metals.

**Copper and zinc**, on a small number of higher-end and heritage properties.
These patina by design, and the patina is the finish. Aggressive cleaning takes
it off unevenly and the result is worse than what you started with. Cleaned very
conservatively, and we will tell you plainly what we are not going to attempt.
