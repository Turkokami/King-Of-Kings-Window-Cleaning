## What cleaning will and will not change

**Comes off:** organic growth in seams and around fasteners, moss and lichen,
needle and leaf debris, streaking, general atmospheric film, salt deposit, pollen,
and most tannin staining.

**Improves:** a chalked surface loses its loose powder and looks considerably
better, and the colour reads truer.

**Does not change:** fading, chalking already in the coating, scratches, dents,
oil-canning, existing rust, or a coating that has failed. None of those are dirt.

The honest framing is that cleaning a metal roof is preservation as much as
appearance. Removing organic material and salt from the details is what stops
small problems becoming corrosion, and it is why a roof that gets cleaned on a
sensible cycle reaches the service life the manufacturer had in mind.

## Metal on agricultural and outbuildings

A large share of the metal roofing in Whatcom and Skagit County is not on houses.
Barns, machine sheds, riding arenas, shops and pole buildings across the valleys
are overwhelmingly exposed-fastener corrugated or ribbed steel, often on large,
simple, shallow-pitched planes.

Those roofs are simpler to clean and have their own concerns. Fastener count is
high, so washer condition matters more. Spans are long, so foot traffic is more
constrained. Agricultural exposure — dust, ammonia in livestock buildings,
fertiliser and chemical drift — is harder on coatings than residential exposure.
And the roofs are frequently large enough that condition monitoring has real
value, because a small fastener problem found early on a barn roof is a very
different number from the same problem found late.
