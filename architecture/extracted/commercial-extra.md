## What commercial glass gets dirty with

Different inputs from a house, and knowing them explains the frequency
recommendations above.

**Hand contact.** The dominant factor on entrance glass and the reason it needs
the shortest interval. Fingerprints, palm marks, pushed doors, children at
window height, and dogs. It concentrates in a band at hand and nose height and it
is the most visible dirt on any commercial building because it is directly in
front of a customer's face.

**Road film.** Any frontage on a busy street collects a fine oily deposit of
brake dust, exhaust particulate and tyre residue, thrown up as spray in wet
weather. Along Meridian, Guide Meridian, Samish Way, State Street and the highway
frontages, this is a genuine soiling load rather than a nuisance.

**Grease and cooking film**, in food service. It migrates further through a space
than people expect, it is adherent, and it holds everything else that lands on
it. Restaurant glass needs both a shorter interval and a different approach from
a dry retail unit.

**Interior condensation film**, in humid spaces — commercial kitchens, gyms,
laundries, pools, and any building with marginal ventilation.

**Salt and spray**, at the marine edge in Blaine, Birch Bay and Anacortes, and on
buildings fronting the water in Fairhaven and along the Bellingham waterfront.

**Construction dust** from nearby projects, which can undo a clean in a week and
is worth scheduling around rather than fighting.

**Signage adhesive and promotional vinyl residue** on display glass — a common
retail problem when seasonal graphics come down.

**Hard water spotting** from irrigation heads hitting frontage glass, which
behaves the same on a storefront as on a house and is fixed the same way: move
the head.

## Seasonal rhythm for commercial buildings

Commercial glass does not have the same two-visits-a-year pattern a house does,
but the year still has a shape.

**January and February** are when interior condensation film is at its worst and
when the little available daylight matters most. Offices notice their glass in
February more than at any other time.

**March into May** brings the pollen load, which lands on frontage glass as
visibly as it lands anywhere else, and the first properly bright days — which is
when every business owner suddenly sees their own windows.

**Summer** is the quiet season for soiling and the busy season for hospitality
frontages, patio glass and anywhere with outdoor seating.

**September and October** bring the return of wet weather and road spray, and
this is the sensible moment to get the entrance walkways and steps done before
they become slippery — see [pressure washing](/services/pressure-washing/).

**November and December** are retail's most visible weeks, and it is worth having
the frontage schedule tightened rather than relaxed through them. It is also when
promotional vinyl goes up and comes down, leaving adhesive residue behind.

For most commercial clients the practical answer is a steady route with a small
seasonal adjustment rather than a wholly different plan four times a year.

## The commercial market here, honestly

Whatcom and Skagit are not a high-rise market and the commercial stock reflects
that: predominantly two- to four-storey buildings, a lot of single-storey retail
and light industrial, historic frontages in downtown Bellingham, Fairhaven,
Lynden and La Conner, and newer suburban retail along the arterial corridors.

That shape suits pole-based work well. It also means the realistic competition
for this work is not a national contractor — it is either a smaller local
operator or, very often, nobody at all, because the business is doing it in-house
badly or not doing it.

Historic frontages deserve a specific mention. Older commercial glazing in
downtown Bellingham, Fairhaven and the small-town main streets often has original
timber frames, painted transoms, leaded or textured glass, and hardware that has
been painted over several times. It cleans beautifully and it needs a lighter
hand and more time than a modern aluminium storefront. It is worth telling us
what you have when you ask for a quote.
