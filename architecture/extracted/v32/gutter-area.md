## Different across the service area

Tree cover and rainfall are not uniform across the two counties, and the schedule
should not be either.

In the **foothills** — Deming, Acme, Van Zandt, Maple Falls, Glacier and Kendall —
rainfall is substantially higher than on the flats, tree cover is dense conifer,
and elevation adds freeze events. This is the heaviest gutter maintenance in the
service area by a wide margin, and it is the part of the region where a missed
autumn clean reliably causes damage.

Around **Lake Whatcom** — Sudden Valley and Geneva — dense second-growth cover and
constant shade mean heavy needle load plus moss establishing in the gutters
themselves. There is also a watershed dimension: what runs off roofs in this
catchment matters, and it constrains what can responsibly be used near a gutter
here.

On the **agricultural flats** through Lynden, Everson, Nooksack, Mount Vernon and
Burlington, tree cover is lighter and the debris load is lower — but field dust
in the dry weeks builds a silt layer faster than anywhere else, and silt is what
holds water and grows moss.

At the **marine edge** — Blaine, Birch Bay, Semiahmoo, Anacortes and the islands —
debris volume is often modest but salt exposure is constant, which is hard on
older steel gutters and on fixings.
