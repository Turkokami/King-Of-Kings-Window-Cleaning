## Commercial and agricultural arrays

Larger installations are a growing part of this work in both counties — barn and
shop roofs on farms through the Nooksack and Skagit valleys, commercial buildings
in Bellingham, Ferndale and Burlington, and school and municipal arrays.

Two things differ from residential. The first is that the economics change: on an
array large enough to matter, a few percent of recovered output is a real number
rather than a rounding error, and cleaning moves from discretionary maintenance
to scheduled maintenance with a defensible business case.

The second is that agricultural arrays soil faster and harder than anything else
we see. Field dust during dry work weeks, particulate from equipment, and in
livestock areas an ammonia-laden deposit that is genuinely adherent and does not
rinse. Farm arrays that get cleaned annually often should be cleaned twice.

Scheduled contracts are quoted per visit with the frequency set by the site
rather than by a default.
