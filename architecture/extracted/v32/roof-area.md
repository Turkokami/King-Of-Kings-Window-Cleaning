## Roofs vary across the service area more than anything else does

Of everything we do, roof growth is the service where location matters most.

In the **foothills and the Mount Baker corridor** — Deming, Acme, Van Zandt,
Kendall, Maple Falls, Glacier — rainfall is much higher than on the flats, tree
cover is dense, and roofs stay wet. Growth here is thick moss rather than light
algae, cycles are short, and roofs that go unmaintained can accumulate cushions
several inches deep in the valleys.

Around **Lake Whatcom**, in Sudden Valley and Geneva, dense second-growth conifer
and permanent shade produce the same heavy moss loading, with the watershed
constraint on top of it.

In **Bellingham proper** the picture varies neighbourhood by neighbourhood —
mature-tree neighbourhoods like Edgemoor, Fairhaven, Silver Beach and Whatcom
Falls carry considerably more shade and growth than the open newer developments
at Cordata or Barkley.

On the **agricultural flats** through Lynden, Everson, Mount Vernon and
Burlington, roofs are more exposed and dry faster. Algae streaking is the more
common presentation, moss the less common.

At the **marine edge** — Blaine, Birch Bay, Anacortes, Guemes and Samish Island —
salt is the additional factor, and it is hard on metal roofs and fixings in a way
it is not inland.
