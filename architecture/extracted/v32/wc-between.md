## Between cleans

A few things genuinely extend the life of a clean, and one thing that quietly
ruins it.

The one that ruins it is **sprinklers**. An irrigation head that throws onto the
glass every morning, on a property with hard water, will spot windows faster than
anything the weather does. It dries in the sun, the mineral stays, and it repeats
daily. Adjusting the head is free and it is the single highest-return thing most
homeowners can do to their windows.

**Trim back what touches the glass.** Shrubs and climbers against a window hold
moisture on the pane and the frame, keep the corner damp, and drag dirt across
the glass every time the wind blows.

**Clear the tracks occasionally.** A vacuum crevice tool along the bottom track a
couple of times a year keeps grit out of the rollers and the weatherstripping.
This is maintenance for the window, not for the view.

**Deal with interior condensation.** Steam from cooking and bathing settles on
cold glass and leaves a film that builds all winter. A little ventilation while
cooking does more for the interior of your windows than wiping them does.

**Leave the pressure washer alone.** A pressure washer aimed at glass or at a
window frame drives water past the seals and into the frame, and on an older
sealed unit it is a good way to turn a clean into a glazing bill.

What does *not* help, despite being everywhere online: newspaper, vinegar and
dish soap in various combinations. Newspaper ink is not what it was and modern
paper falls apart wet. Vinegar will lift light mineral film but does nothing for
bonded film and smells for a day. Dish soap contains additives that leave a
residue attracting dust, which is why home-washed windows so often look worse a
week later than they did before.
