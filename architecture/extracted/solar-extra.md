## Why the low sun angle here makes soiling worse

A detail specific to this latitude, and it explains why Pacific Northwest arrays
accumulate an edge band that arrays further south largely do not.

Solar panels are installed at a tilt chosen to catch the sun. The steeper the
tilt, the more readily water, dust and debris run off the glass under gravity —
a self-cleaning effect that is a genuine part of how arrays are expected to
maintain themselves.

Two things work against that here. First, a great many residential arrays in this
region are **flush-mounted to the roof plane**, taking whatever pitch the roof
happens to be, rather than being angled optimally on tilt frames. On a shallow
roof that is a shallow panel, and a shallow panel sheds far less readily.

Second, the wet season here is also the **low-light season**. Between November
and February the sun barely gets above the ridgeline in much of the county, and
the array is producing very little regardless. The rain falling in those months
does rinse the glass — but the material it carries has all winter to settle
against the bottom frame, and there is no strong summer sun drying and shrinking
the deposit off. By the time production genuinely matters again in March and
April, the band is established, and it is immediately followed by pollen season.

The practical consequence is that the single most useful clean of the year is the
one in late spring, and its value is concentrated in the months right after it.

## Panels are not all the same glass

Not a detail most homeowners need, but it shapes how the work is done.

**Monocrystalline and polycrystalline framed panels** — the overwhelming majority
of residential installs here. Tempered front glass, aluminium frame, the raised
perimeter lip described above, and an anti-reflective coating. Standard method.

**Frameless and dual-glass panels**, more common on newer commercial installs,
have no perimeter lip — so no edge band, and considerably less to clean. They are
also more sensitive to point loads, which reinforces the never-walk-on-them rule.

**Textured or patterned front glass**, used on some panels to reduce glare and
improve light capture at shallow angles, holds soiling in the texture more than
smooth glass does and takes longer to clean properly.

**Thin-film panels** turn up occasionally on older or specialist installs. Same
principles, gentler still.

**Solar hot-water collectors** are not photovoltaic panels and are a different
thing entirely — glazed collectors clean much like glass, evacuated tube arrays
have their own fragility. We will tell you which you have if you are not sure;
plenty of people are not.

## Skylights, sunrooms and glass roofs

Frequently the same visit, because the equipment and the access are already there
and the surfaces have the same problem: overhead glass that nobody can reach and
that shows every mark because you look up through it against the sky.

Skylights collect debris in the well around the frame and are one of the more
neglected surfaces on a house — they are also, along with solar panels, a place
where moss establishes at the frame junction and holds moisture against a seal.
Conservatory and sunroom glazing is the same story at larger scale.

Both are quoted as part of [window cleaning](/services/window-cleaning/), but if
you are booking a panel clean it is worth asking, because the marginal cost of
adding them while the pole system is set up is small.
