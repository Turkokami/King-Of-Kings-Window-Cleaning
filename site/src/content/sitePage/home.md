## The county does the damage, the schedule should answer it

Exterior cleaning in Whatcom and Skagit County is not the same job it is in a dry
climate, and the difference is not marketing. Wet air off the bay, tree cover on
three sides of most neighbourhoods, and a long grey season mean organic growth
gets a foothold on any surface that stays damp. North-facing walls hold mildew.
Roofs grow moss in the shade line. Gutters carry needles rather than leaves, and
they carry them for months.

That is the whole argument for cleaning on a local schedule rather than a generic
one, and it is the reason this site is organised by surface and by town rather
than by a list of services with a phone number underneath.

## Why the surface matters more than the service name

A composition asphalt shingle, a cedar shake, a standing seam metal roof and a
vinyl wall all fail in different ways, and all four fail faster under the wrong
cleaning method. Pressure on a composition roof takes the granules with it, and
those granules are the part doing the work. Cedar needs to dry. Metal has coatings
that some chemistry strips. The [surface library](/surface-library/) sets out what
each one is and how it should be treated, because the honest answer to "can you
clean that" is sometimes "yes, but not the way you have seen it done".

## Wash water, and why we bring our own

Where the water goes is regulated here, and not identically from town to town.
Bellingham's stormwater chapter admits nothing but stormwater to the drain;
Ferndale's code expressly permits detergent-free external wash down. The
practical effect is that the same pole rinse is unremarkable in one town and not
exempt ten miles away. We carry our own water and filtration, which means we can
work at a property with the supply shut off and we are not putting hard well
water back onto glass we have just cleaned. The
[local rules are written up here](/compliance/).
