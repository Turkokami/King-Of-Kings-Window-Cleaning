Ferndale sits on the Nooksack River a short run north of Bellingham on the
freeway, and it is tempting to treat it as an outer edge of the bigger city. For
exterior work that would be a mistake. The houses are younger, the water comes out
of the ground rather than out of a lake, an industrial shoreline lies to the west,
and the city writes its own rules about where wash water may go.

What follows is the overview: what is standing in Ferndale, what the setting does
to it, and how each of our services plays out here.

## What kind of houses does Ferndale have?

Mostly detached family homes, and a surprising number of them recent. The American
Community Survey puts the median year built for Ferndale's housing at 1998, which
is young for a town whose name became official in 1876. Of about 6,200 housing
units, 1,690 went up in 2010 or later, which means more than a quarter of the
town's homes are less than twenty years old.

The same survey counts 3,967 single-family detached homes, 411 mobile homes and 591
units in buildings of twenty to forty-nine apartments. Detached houses are the
everyday call, but they are not the only one.

That mix shapes the work in a few specific ways:

- **Young housing usually means original roofs and cladding.** That is
  good news for the materials and it means early growth can be dealt with before
  it becomes a restoration job.
- **Manufactured homes need a lighter hand.** Thin skins, exposed seams and
  shallow roof pitches do not reward force, and reaching them safely is a
  different exercise from a two-storey house.
- **The older stock is still here.** The Hovander Homestead house, a two-storey
  home with Scandinavian design elements, was finished by 1903, and Pioneer Park
  holds thirteen pioneer cabins moved there between 1940 and 1989. The town's
  building record runs well past the subdivisions.
- **Apartment buildings are quoted as their own thing,** because access, height
  and scheduling around residents all differ from a single house.

Interstate 5 was completed in 1970 and drew new residents to Ferndale, and in
practical terms the building has not stopped since.

## What does the setting do to a Ferndale exterior?

Three influences land on houses here, and each leaves its own signature.

### The river

The Nooksack runs right through town. On 16 November 2021 it flooded parts
of Ferndale, including Main Street near the Pioneer Bridge and the I-5 on-ramp at
Main Street, according to local news coverage from that day. We are not going to
tell you what the river does on your particular street, because we have not
measured it. What is generally true of low, wet ground is that walls and roof edges
near it dry slowly, and a surface that stays damp is a surface that grows things.

### Cherry Point

West of town, Cherry Point hosts two working refineries: the BP Cherry Point
Refinery and the Phillips 66 Ferndale Refinery. The Alcoa Intalco aluminium smelter
that stood between them stopped operating in 2020 and is being demolished; the
work was roughly 45% done in March 2026. On exposed glass and siding toward that
side of town we see a fine, even film that is different from ordinary grime. What
is in it, we cannot say, and our
[Cherry Point industrial fallout](/surface-library/cherry-point-industrial-fallout/)
profile explains why we draw the line there.

### The wet months

Like the rest of the Whatcom lowlands, Ferndale spends a long stretch of the year
damp and grey. Shaded roof faces, north walls and the inside of gutters are the
places that feel it first, because they rarely get enough sun to dry out between
one weather system and the next.

## Where does Ferndale's water come from, and why would a cleaner care?

Ferndale draws its city water from three groundwater wells: two in the
Vashon-Olympia aquifer and one in the deeper Possession-Whidbey aquifer. Two of
them are easy to place — the Douglas Well just off Douglas Avenue and the Shop Well
in the Public Works yard off Legoe Avenue. Until December 2011 the city bought
Nooksack River water through Whatcom County PUD No. 1 instead.

The city added reverse osmosis in October 2014 to bring down the hardness of its
well water, and according to its 2025 water quality report the plant has run full
reverse osmosis since July 2025, with softening and chlorination as well.

That matters to anyone who sprays water on a house. Water that dries on glass or
dark trim leaves behind whatever minerals it was carrying, and harder water leaves
more. Softer city supply is a genuine help. The city does not publish a hardness
figure in that report, so neither do we, and a property that is not on city
supply is a question worth asking at the quote.

## How do windows fare in Ferndale?

Glass is where every one of the influences above shows up first: the slow-drying
damp in the frame corners, the even haze on glass toward Cherry Point, and any mineral
left behind by rinse water. We carry our own purified water for the pole work, so
what dries on the upper glass is nothing at all. The detail, including how we

## What do Ferndale gutters need?

A clear run from the top corner to the ground, and a downspout that actually
delivers the water somewhere useful. On a young house the gutters are usually
sound; the risk is a clog nobody has noticed because the house has never needed
its gutters looked at before. On lower ground, where water already has nowhere
fast to go, a spilling gutter adds to the problem at the foot of the wall. The
specifics are on
[Ferndale gutter cleaning](/locations/ferndale/gutter-cleaning/).

## What about roofs?

With so many houses built since the late 1990s, the typical Ferndale roof is
composition shingle somewhere in its first life, and that is exactly the stage at
which it is worth protecting. Soft washing kills algae, moss and lichen with a
low-pressure solution rather than knocking them off with force, which keeps the
protective granules where the manufacturer put them. Timing, method and cost
drivers are set out on
[roof cleaning in Ferndale](/locations/ferndale/roof-cleaning/).

## And the siding?

Green on the north wall, a grey dulling on the Cherry Point side of town, and splash along the bottom course
where rain bounces off the ground. That is the usual Ferndale pattern, and it all
comes off with a soft wash: a cleaning solution at garden-hose pressure, time to
work, then a thorough rinse. Siding is never pressure washed, whatever it is made
of. The page on
[house washing in Ferndale](/locations/ferndale/house-washing/) covers vinyl,
fiber cement, wood and manufactured-home skins in turn.

## Which runoff rules apply to a Ferndale address?

It depends on which side of the city limits the house sits, and a Ferndale
mailing address does not settle that.

**Inside city limits,** Ferndale Municipal Code 13.35.020(B)(4) lists "routine
external building wash down that does not use detergents" as an allowable
discharge. A plain-water rinse therefore sits inside a written allowance. Anything
using a cleaning solution — every soft wash, most roof treatments — does not, and
that water is contained and directed to vegetation instead of a drain.

**In the unincorporated Urban Growth Area,** Whatcom County's 2025 Stormwater
Management Program includes the Ferndale UGA in its NPDES permit area, which puts
those properties under the county's illicit-discharge rules in WCC chapter 16.36.

We check which applies while quoting, not on the morning of the job. The full
comparison with Bellingham, sources included, is on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).
