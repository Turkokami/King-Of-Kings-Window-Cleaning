Mount Vernon is a river town. The historic downtown sits within the Skagit
River delta, the city built its downtown floodwall in phases between 2010
and 2018, and the valley around it grows about 1,000 acres of tulips and
daffodils each year.

For a house, that setting means damp ground, long wet winters and a lot of
growth on anything that stays in shade. It is the southern end of our regular
route, and we treat it as its own market rather than an extension of Whatcom.

## What are Mount Vernon's homes like?

A broad mix, with a solid core of single-family houses. The census counts
13,623 housing units, about 61% of them single-family detached, and 833 mobile
homes. The median build year is 1989, but roughly one home in ten (1,309 units)
predates 1940, which gives the city far more old painted wood than a quick
look at the median would suggest.

City documents name two areas in particular: the historic downtown district and
the Eaglemont community development, which contains Beaver Pond. Across the city, about 60% of occupied homes are lived in by their owners,
which leaves a sizeable rented share. If you let a house here, we can schedule
exterior work around your tenants and send you the result, since the owner is
not always the one standing in the driveway.

What that means on a quote:

- **Pre-war houses** often have painted lap siding, original trim and older
  glass, all of which want a light hand
- **Homes from recent decades**, where half the stock falls around a 1989 median,
  are mostly straightforward soft-wash and pole work
- **Manufactured homes** get the same methods, with the roof assessed before
  anyone steps on it

## How wet is Mount Vernon, and what does the river add?

The Mt Vernon 3 WNW weather station averaged 32.58 inches of precipitation a
year across its 1956 to 2005 record. November was the wettest month at 4.48
inches and July the driest at 1.16 inches, which tells you when growth builds and
when surfaces finally dry out.

The Skagit adds its own pressure. It has flooded numerous times since 1897, and
in December 2025 an atmospheric river pushed it to a record crest of 37.73 feet
in Mount Vernon. The city's parks plan marks sizable flood-prone areas across
most of downtown and the west side, along the river and the Kulshan Creek,
Carpenter Creek and Britt Slough corridors. The river also makes a sharp bend
here, known as the Avon Turn.

Low, wet ground keeps walls and concrete damp longer after each storm. That is
the condition algae and moss thrive in, and it is why north walls, shaded
driveways and lower siding courses on this side of the county often look older
than the house is.

## What happened on one Mount Vernon driveway?

In August 2026 we pressure washed the front entry, garage driveway and walkway
at a Mount Vernon home. The concrete had gone almost black with organic growth,
the kind of coating that builds on damp concrete over several wet seasons.
Before and after photographs were taken on that job.

It is a useful example because many people assume dark concrete is stained for
good. Growth sits on the surface and in the pores, and a rotary surface cleaner
with a proper rinse takes it off evenly, without the stripes a hand wand leaves.
More on how we approach flatwork is on our [pressure
washing](/services/pressure-washing/) page, which also covers patios, steps and
decks.

## Window cleaning

Our [window cleaning](/services/window-cleaning/) includes interior and exterior
glass, screens and tracks. Ground-floor panes get a scrubber and squeegee; upper
storeys and glass above decks or conservatories get a water-fed pole.

That pole runs on purified water from the truck. Mount Vernon's supply is Skagit
PUD's Judy Reservoir system, fed by four streams on Cultus Mountain with a Skagit
River diversion as backup, but we could not reach a published hardness figure
for it and will not invent one. Carrying our own water keeps the question off
your glass entirely.

On older homes with original windows, we slow down around glazing putty and
frames that have been through many decades of weather.

## Gutter cleaning

Gutters are where a wet November shows up first. [Gutter
cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning/) here means scooping every run by hand,
flushing the fine sediment, and running water through each downspout to prove it
drains.

On low ground, a blocked downspout does more than overflow. It drops roof water
right beside the foundation, where the soil is often already saturated after a
storm. We would rather find the clog in October than have you find it in the
basement in December.

Most houses here are well served by one cleaning a year in early autumn. Homes
under big trees may want a spring visit as well.

## Roof cleaning and moss removal

A roof in this valley spends much of the year damp, and shaded pitches grow moss
steadily. [Roof cleaning](/services/roof-cleaning/) is a soft wash matched to the
roofing material, and [moss removal](/services/moss-removal/) lifts the growth by
hand before treatment, so we are not driving water up under shingles with a
pressure wand.

We set the method by what the roof is made of, and we tell you on the quote if a
roof looks like it needs a roofer's attention rather than a clean. We are
cleaners, not inspectors, and we will say so rather than guess at what is
underneath.

## House washing and siding

Siding on the shaded side of a Mount Vernon house often carries a green or
black film by late winter. [House washing](/services/house-washing/) is a
low-pressure soft wash: the right solution for the siding, a short dwell, and a
gentle rinse from the top down.

On the city's older painted homes we test a small area before committing to the
whole wall, and we point out chalking or peeling paint before the work begins.
Vinyl and fiber cement on newer houses take the same approach at a different
strength.

## Solar panel cleaning

Panels in a wet valley collect a mix of pollen, road film and organic growth
around the frame edges. [Solar panel
cleaning](/services/solar-panel-cleaning/) uses purified water and soft brushes,
with no abrasives and nobody walking on the array.

Late spring, after the pollen, is a sensible time for panels. If your monitoring
shows output drifting down with no obvious cause, dirt is a quick thing to check
off before calling the installer.

## When is the right time of year to book here?

The station record gives a fair guide. With November the wettest month and July
the driest, the valley has a clear drying season, and most exterior work goes
better inside it.

- **March to May** suits roof and moss work, house washing and windows. Growth is
  easy to see after winter, and walls have a chance to dry once the wash is done.
- **Late spring** is the moment for solar panels, once pollen has mostly fallen.
- **July and August** are good for concrete, because a slab dries quickly and the
  clean result is obvious within the day.
- **September and early October** are for gutters, ahead of the November rain,
  and a second round of glass before the short days.

December and January are the months we would rather look than clean. A roof that
is green and swollen in the wet is easy to assess, and the quote you get then can
be scheduled for the first dry stretch of spring.

## What does Mount Vernon's code say about wash water?

It is strict and specific. Chapter 13.33 of the Mount Vernon Municipal Code
covers the drainage system, and three sections matter to exterior cleaning:

1. **MVMC 13.33.080** prohibits illicit discharges to the public drainage
   system, and lists soap, detergent, household and commercial cleaning
   materials and steam-cleaning waste among them.
2. **MVMC 13.33.080(E)** exempts street and sidewalk wash water and routine
   external building wash-down that uses no detergent.
3. **MVMC 13.33.150** makes it a violation to let soaps, detergents or washing
   wastes enter any private drainage system as well as the public one.

That last point is easy to miss. A private yard drain counts, so a crew that rinses soap into it is
breaking the rule even if the street grate is untouched.

On the job this means plain water wherever it does the work, drains in the work
zone blocked before any solution goes on, and rinse steered to grass or beds.
Our [wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/)
page lays out how we handle it.

## Which neighbouring towns do you serve?

Mount Vernon is part of a Skagit leg that also takes in Burlington and
Anacortes. The water connects them too: Skagit PUD bought the systems serving
Burlington, Mount Vernon and Sedro-Woolley back in 1939, and the City of
Anacortes runs its regional water treatment plant on the east bank of the Skagit
near Mount Vernon.

From Whatcom County the same crews come down on a set rotation, so a Skagit
booking slots into a planned day rather than waiting for a gap. If your address
sits just outside the city and you are unsure whether we cover it, ask and we
will tell you plainly.
