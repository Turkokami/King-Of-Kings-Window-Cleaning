Sudden Valley is a lake community before it is anything else. Close to three
thousand homes sit beside Lake Whatcom here, most of them lived in by the people
who own them, and the water that runs off their roofs, gutters and driveways is
headed for the same lake that fills their kitchen taps.

That single fact shapes how we clean a house in Sudden Valley more than the
siding, the roofing or the age of the glass does. It is why this page, and the
four service pages that sit under it, spend as much time on where the rinse goes
as on what comes off the walls.

## What is the housing like in Sudden Valley?

Mostly detached houses, mostly owner-occupied, built in two clear bursts. Census
estimates count 2,671 of Sudden Valley's 2,985 housing units as detached
single-family homes, roughly 89%, and record no mobile homes at all. About 93% of
occupied homes are lived in by their owners, which matters to us: the person
booking the work is usually the person who will still be looking up at that roof
a decade from now.

The building history shows plainly in the numbers:

- Development began after Sudden Valley Inc. bought 1,200 acres in 1968, and the
  community association took control in December 1976.
- The 1970s alone produced 993 of today's homes.
- A second wave between 2000 and 2009 added 844 more.
- Only 105 have gone up since 2010.

The median year built is 1991, but that figure sits in the gap between two peaks
rather than describing a typical house. For exterior cleaning the split is
useful. A first-wave home is now around fifty years old, and it may or may not be
wearing a replacement roof, replacement windows and siding that has been painted
several times; the only way to know is to stand in front of it. A home from the
2000s is usually on its first or second round of most exterior materials, and
the conversation tends to be about keeping things in shape rather than bringing
them back.

## Why does Lake Whatcom change how the work is done?

Because the lake is the water supply. The Lake Whatcom Water and Sewer District
draws from Basin 3 of the lake to serve Sudden Valley, Geneva and parts of North
Shore and South Bay. Across all its users, the lake provides drinking water to
more than 120,000 people, which Whatcom County puts at about half of everyone
living in the county.

The lake also carries a documented water-quality problem. Washington placed it
on the state's 303(d) list of polluted waters in 1998 after it failed the
dissolved-oxygen standard, and it now sits under a cleanup plan, a TMDL, for
phosphorus and bacteria. Whatcom County describes the Sudden Valley Community
Association as the largest development anywhere in the watershed, and the county
is building stormwater improvements inside Sudden Valley specifically to reduce
the phosphorus reaching the lake.

Put those facts side by side and the careless habits of this trade look
different. Gutter sludge tipped into a ditch, a soft-wash rinse left to run down
the driveway, a mossy path hosed toward the nearest grate: each is a small
delivery of organic matter, and possibly cleaning solution, into a lake people
drink from and that is already short of oxygen. On a Sudden Valley property we
decide where every gallon of rinse is going before a single surface gets wet.

## What does tree cover do to houses here?

It keeps them damp, and it keeps them supplied with debris. A good many Sudden
Valley homes sit under a canopy, and a roof in shade dries slowly after rain,
which is exactly the condition moss and algae need to settle in. The same trees
drop needles, twigs and cones into the gutters over the whole year instead of in
one tidy autumn fall.

Elsewhere the usual answer is to take a tree down or limb it back hard. Inside
the Lake Whatcom watershed that is not a casual call: Whatcom County requires a
Tree Removal Permit to remove any tree on a watershed lot, unless the removal is
part of an approved development. For most owners the practical plan is to keep
the canopy and clean on a schedule that matches it. We can tell you what that
schedule looks like for your house. Whether a particular tree can come down is a
question for the county, and we will not pretend otherwise.

## How do we handle gutter cleaning in Sudden Valley?

By hand, with the debris bagged and hauled off rather than dumped at the foot of
the downspout. Under trees, a gutter here can fill faster than the calendar
suggests, and a wet mat of needles lying flat in the trough is invisible from the
lawn. We clear each run, confirm that every downspout actually drains, and tell
you if the water is leaving the pipe somewhere it should not. The details,
including how often a shaded lot usually needs a visit, are on the
[gutter cleaning page for Sudden Valley](/locations/sudden-valley/gutter-cleaning/).

## How do we clean roofs in Sudden Valley?

Low pressure, a solution matched to the roofing material, and a rinse plan that
keeps runoff off the drainage system. Shaded roofs grow moss and the black
streaking caused by algae, and both are treated rather than blasted; a pressure
washer strips granules from composition shingle and splinters cedar. Dead moss
then weathers away over the following weeks. The roof is the largest surface on
the house and it empties straight into the gutters, so it is where watershed care
matters most. The [Sudden Valley roof cleaning page](/locations/sudden-valley/roof-cleaning/)
explains how we handle it.

## What does house washing involve here?

A soft wash that lifts green film from shaded walls without forcing water behind
the siding laps. A fifty-year-old house and a twenty-year-old one may carry very
different cladding, and the solution strength, dwell time and rinse all change
with it. The [house washing page for Sudden Valley](/locations/sudden-valley/house-washing/)
sets out what the job includes and what the county expects to happen to the
wash water afterwards.

## What about window cleaning?

Inside and out, with screens, tracks and sills included, and purified water on
the upper glass so it dries without spotting. Nobody has published a verified
hardness figure for the district's supply, so we carry our own filtered water
rather than depending on your outside tap. If the house looks toward the lake or
the golf course, point out the windows that face the view; those are the ones
worth a second look at the final walk-round. More on the
[Sudden Valley window cleaning page](/locations/sudden-valley/window-cleaning/).

## Which runoff rules apply in Sudden Valley?

Whatcom County's, because Sudden Valley is unincorporated. The City of
Bellingham's stormwater chapter does not reach here, whatever the mailing address
suggests. The county's own guidance on pressure washing and car washing cites WCC
16.36.050 and is plain about the expectation: collect the wash water and keep it
out of storm drains and waterways. Sudden Valley is also one of the areas named
inside the county's NPDES Phase II municipal stormwater permit area, the permit
under which the county manages its drainage system.

The watershed layers more on top. Under WCC 16.32.050, commercial lawn fertiliser
containing any phosphorus at all may not be used on residential lawns in the Lake
Whatcom watershed, which gives a fair sense of how seriously phosphorus is taken.
Ground-disturbing work in the watershed is limited to June 1 through September
30. Cleaning does not dig, but if a gutter visit turns up a downspout that needs
a new drain line, that window is worth knowing about before the repair gets
scheduled.

On the ground, that becomes a short routine:

1. Find the drain inlets near the work area and cover them before anything is
   applied.
2. Steer rinse onto soil and planting instead of onto paving that runs to the
   road.
3. Bag roof and gutter debris and take it away, never rake it into a ditch or the
   woods.
4. Move rinse-heavy work to another day in a downpour, when runoff cannot be
   steered at all.

The wider reasoning, with the relevant codes quoted, is on the
[wash water and storm drains page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Do I need to check with the community association first?

Possibly, and you are the person in a position to find out. Sudden Valley's
association has run the community since December 1976, but we have not been
able to read its current rules, and we are not going to guess at them on a web
page. If you want to be sure whether any part of the work needs sign-off, check
the association's rules or ask its office before you book. We are happy to plan
around whatever answer comes back.
