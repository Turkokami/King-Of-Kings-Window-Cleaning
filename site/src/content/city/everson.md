Everson is a small river town with a big valley around it. Ever Everson, a
Norwegian-born homesteader, settled the site in 1871, and the town that grew up
there still sits on the Nooksack River with berry fields, dairy ground and
potato rows running out from the edge of it. Bellingham is about 15 miles to the
southwest.

For an exterior cleaning crew, that setting decides most of the work before we
unload a ladder. Here is what we see and how we handle it.

## What kind of houses does Everson have?

Mostly detached family homes, and mostly from the last fifty years. Of Everson's
1,074 housing units, 750 are detached single-family houses, which is about 70% of
the town. The rest is a mix, including 110 units in three- and four-unit
buildings and 75 mobile homes.

The building record, decade by decade, looks like this:

- **The 1970s**, with 237 units, the single busiest decade
- **The 1980s**, with 219 more, and the median home here dates from 1987
- **2010 to 2019**, when 173 units went up
- **2020 onward**, with 77 built so far

At the other end, 105 homes date from 1939 or earlier, roughly one in ten.

That spread matters on the ground. A 1970s rambler, a 1980s split-level, a
brand-new build and a pre-war farmhouse all use different materials, carry
different ages of roofing and paint, and tolerate different cleaning methods.
We identify the surface first and pick the method second. About 63% of occupied
homes here are owner-occupied, and the remainder are rented, so we also work for
landlords who want a unit turned around between tenants.

## How does the Nooksack River affect Everson homes?

It floods, and it is watched closely enough to have its own gauge. The National
Weather Service tracks one named "Nooksack River Overflow At SR544 Everson". Its
flood-impact statement says that when the river overtops its banks here, water
crosses Main Street and SR 544, reaches low-lying areas around the City of
Nooksack and can push north toward Sumas.

This is not a new problem. HistoryLink records one of the valley's great floods
around 1885 and a massive flood in 1990.

What that means for an exterior is practical rather than dramatic. River water
carries silt, and on a house in the low ground it leaves a line on siding,
fences and foundations when it drains away. Even homes that never see the water
live in a valley that stays damp through the wet months, and damp ground keeps
the lower courses of siding, the shaded side of a roof and the bottom of a
downspout wetter for longer. We would never tell you your lot floods; we will
simply ask, and plan the job around the answer.

## What does the weather do to siding, roofs and glass?

It keeps them wet. We do not have a rain gauge in Everson to quote, but the
WRCC's Clearbrook cooperative station, a regional station out in the county
rather than in town, averages 46.71 inches of precipitation a year. November and
December each run above six inches there.

Put a long wet season on top of a river valley and you get three familiar
patterns:

1. **Green film on the north and shaded walls** that never quite dry between
   storms from October onward.
2. **Moss and algae on the roof**, starting at the eaves and on slopes that sit
   in the shadow of trees or outbuildings.
3. **Gutters that do their hardest work in the two wettest months**, which is
   exactly when a blockage shows up as water over the front edge.

Farm country adds its own layer. The fields around Everson and Nooksack grow
berries, carrots, potatoes and specialty produce, and there are dairy herds
nearby. Working ground puts dust in the air in the dry months, and that dust
settles on glass and window frames as a film that the first autumn rain turns to
streaks.

## Where does Everson's water come from, and why do we care?

From the ground. The city's supply is the Strandell Well Field, about
three-quarters of a mile southwest of downtown. Deep Well #6 is the main
source, drawing from an aquifer 156 feet down. Wells #4 and #5 tap a shallow,
unconfined glacial sand-and-gravel aquifer and are held as emergency backup.

The same system reaches well beyond the city limits. It supplies the Everson
Water Association and the Hampton Water Association, and runs as far south as
the Hoekema farm on Emerson Road, so plenty of rural addresses around town are
drinking the same well water as Main Street.

An exterior cleaner cares about this for two reasons. The first is glass. The city's latest water quality report gives no hardness figure, and without one we
have no business telling an Everson homeowner their tap water spots windows, so
we sidestep it entirely by carrying filtered water for anything that dries on
glass. The second is the aquifer itself. A shallow sand-and-gravel aquifer is
the kind of ground where what soaks in eventually matters, which is part of why
we keep detergent to the minimum a surface needs and never tip leftover mix on
the ground at the end of a job.

## What changes between an older house and a new build?

More than people expect. A house built before 1939, wherever it stands, often
carries painted wood that has been recoated many times, and old paint chalks, lifts and
flakes under the wrong treatment. Houses of the 1970s and 1980s are where we typically find
original or second-generation composition roofs, aluminium gutters that have
been re-hung more than once, and vinyl or hardboard siding that has had decades
of sun. Homes built since 2010 commonly have fibre cement or newer vinyl,
larger windows and cleaner rooflines, and their problems are younger: builder
film on glass, early algae on the shaded side, and gutters that were never
sized for the trees planted beside them.

None of that is a reason to worry. It is a reason to look closely before
quoting, which is how we work anyway.

## Windows in Everson

A clean window here is mostly about removing a mix of road grime, field dust
and winter film, and then keeping it off. We clean the glass, screens, tracks
and sills, reaching upper panes from the ground with a water-fed pole where we
can. The detailed Everson version, including how often we suggest booking, is on
the [Everson window cleaning page](/locations/everson/window-cleaning/).

Pole work runs on purified water we carry in the truck, for the reasons set out
in the water section above, so nothing from an outdoor tap is left to dry on the
glass.

## Gutters in Everson

The wettest part of the year is also when gutters are most likely to be carrying
a season of needles, leaves and grit. We clear runs by hand, flush every
downspout and check the water actually gets away from the foundation. On a
low-lying lot that last part is not optional; ground that is already saturated
has nowhere to put an overflow. See [gutter cleaning in
Everson](/locations/everson/gutter-cleaning/) for the full scope.

## Roofs in Everson

Moss and algae are the main event on Everson roofs, and the cure is chemistry
and patience, not pressure. We soft wash composition shingle, which is what most
homes of this age carry, and treat cedar and metal roofs with the methods those
surfaces need. The [Everson roof cleaning page](/locations/everson/roof-cleaning/)
walks through how we do it and why the result keeps improving after we leave.

## Siding in Everson

A low-pressure soft wash lifts algae, mildew and road film without driving water
behind vinyl laps or stripping paint from older wood. Mobile homes and
multi-unit buildings get the same careful approach as a detached house, with the
setup adjusted for access. Everything specific to this town is on the
[Everson house washing page](/locations/everson/house-washing/).

## Are there rules about wash water in Everson?

Probably, but we have not been able to read them, and we will not pretend
otherwise. Everson's municipal code was not available when this page was
written, so we are making no claim about what it says on stormwater or storm
drains. Rules genuinely differ between jurisdictions in Whatcom County, sometimes
sharply between towns a short drive apart.

What does not change is how we work: rinse water is directed to lawn or
planting where it can soak in, drains are protected when a job sits close to
one, and we keep detergent use to what the surface needs. Our
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/) page
explains the approach and cites the codes we have been able to check.

One local detail is worth knowing. The City of Everson runs a wastewater
treatment plant that serves both Everson and the City of Nooksack. That is
sewer infrastructure. Where Everson's street drains lead is something we could
not confirm, so on every job we treat a storm grate as a direct line to open
water.
