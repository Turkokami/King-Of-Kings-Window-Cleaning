The gutters on this Bellingham house had stopped being gutters. They were
troughs full of wet leaves and fir needles, and the downspouts underneath them
were blocked as well.

That combination is the one homeowners tend to miss. A gutter can be cleared
along its length and still fail to drain, because the vertical outlets are what
carry the water away from the house.

## What was actually in there

Leaves, fir needles and the usual organic debris had compacted into the channels
until there was very little room left for water to move.

Wet needles are the problem material in this part of the county. They knit
together into a mat that water cannot pass through, and unlike leaves they arrive
steadily all year rather than in one autumn drop, so a system can silt up between
one visit and the next without anything dramatic happening.

The downspouts had gone the same way. Debris works down to the outlet, packs at
the narrowest point and stays there.

## How the job was done

- **Removed the debris by hand.** Wet material came out of the channels rather
  than being blown along them — blowing packed debris mostly relocates it into
  the downspouts.
- **Cleared the downspouts.** Treated as part of the same job, not an extra.
  Clearing the trough alone leaves the drainage problem exactly where it was.
- **Flushed the system** with water, moving the remaining loose material through
  and out.
- **Verified the flow.** The work was not finished until water had run through
  the cleared gutters and out of the downspouts.

## Why the flush is the part that matters

The objective was never to make the gutter look better from the ground. It was to
restore an unobstructed path from the roofline to the outlet.

Those are different jobs, and only one of them can be checked. Appearance tells
you the surface debris has gone. Running water through the system tells you the
whole path is open, including the parts nobody can see — the outlet, the elbow
and the length of pipe below it.

That is why the last photographs from this job are of water having somewhere to
go, rather than of a tidy-looking trough.

## What it looked like at the end

The accumulated material was out, the downspouts were open, and the system had
been flushed and checked.

For a property with this much tree cover, that is a maintenance cycle rather than
a one-off repair — the needles will start again, because the trees have not moved.
What changes is that the system starts the wet season able to cope with them.
