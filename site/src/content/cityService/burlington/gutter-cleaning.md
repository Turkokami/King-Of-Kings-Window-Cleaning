A gutter in Burlington has an easy summer and a hard winter. For a few dry
months it sits nearly idle. Then the autumn rain arrives, the leaves come down
at the same time, and a run that looked fine in August is holding water and
spilling over its front lip by the middle of November.

The job is to have it empty, flowing and pointed at the right place before that
happens. Here is how we approach it in this town specifically.

## Why do Burlington gutters overflow?

Mostly because of timing. The leaf fall and the start of the wet season overlap
here, so a gutter that fills with leaves in October meets its heaviest rain
only a few weeks later, while the debris is still wet and packed in the trough.

The ground plays a part as well. Burlington sits on the flat floodplain where the
Skagit River turns southwest, and on level ground water that spills from a
gutter does not run away downhill the way it might on a sloping lot in the
hills. It pools against the wall, soaks the bed along the foundation, and
splashes mud back up onto the lowest courses of siding.

Then there is what falls into the gutter besides leaves. On an open valley lot,
wind-blown dust and fine soil settle in the trough through the dry months and
turn to a dense sludge once it rains. Moss shed from the roof adds to it all
year. None of these alone is dramatic, but together they are why a gutter here
can block without a single tree anywhere near the house.

## What does the local rainfall pattern mean for your gutters?

Burlington does not have a long-term weather station, so we use the one closest
to it, Mt Vernon 3 WNW. Between 1956 and 2005 that station logged an average of
4.48 inches in November and just 1.16 inches in July. Its yearly average was
32.58 inches.

The figure that matters to a gutter is the gap between those two months. A
system that coped perfectly well through summer can be overwhelmed in its first
real autumn storm, and the first sign is usually water sheeting over the front
edge at a corner or above a doorway. If you see that, the trough or the drop
below it is already partly blocked.

## How often should a Burlington gutter be cleaned?

Once a year is the baseline, and for a house with nothing overhanging the roof it
is often enough. The single clean should land between leaf drop and the heavy
rain, which in Burlington generally means the last days of October or the first
week of November.

From there the schedule moves with the lot rather than the calendar:

- **Open lot, no trees over the roof:** one autumn visit a year usually keeps
  the system flowing.
- **A tree or two within reach of the eaves:** an autumn clean plus a late
  winter check, because storms bring down twigs, needles and cones after the
  main leaf fall is over.
- **Heavy cover on one or more sides:** two to three visits, set by what the
  trees drop and when.
- **A roof carrying moss:** expect the gutters to refill steadily until the roof
  itself is treated.

After the first visit we can tell you which of these your house falls into,
because we will have seen what was in the gutter and how much of it there was.
A trough half full of seed pods tells a different story from one lined with
roof grit, and the answer sets how soon we should come back.

The one season we do not push is midsummer. Gutters in July are generally dry
and quiet, and cleaning them then just means cleaning them again once autumn
has had its say.

## What is included in a gutter clean?

Every visit covers the same core work, whatever the size of the house:

- **Every run cleared by hand**, corner to corner, including the inside corners
  where debris packs tightest
- **Debris bagged and removed**, not blown across the lawn or rinsed into the
  street
- **Each downspout flushed** from the top and checked for flow at the outlet
- **Outlet screens and elbows** cleared where they are accessible
- **The roof edge checked** for anything loose that will slide into the gutter
  next week
- **Photographs** of anything worth your attention, taken on the day

Gutter guards, where fitted, are lifted and cleaned beneath if they come off
without damage. If they are fixed in a way that makes removal a risk, we say so
before starting rather than prying them off.

## How are the gutters actually cleared?

By hand and by ladder, for the most part. A scoop and a gloved hand are slower
than a leaf blower, but they remove the compacted sludge at the bottom of the
trough that a blower skips over, and they put the debris in a bag instead of on
your roof, your car or your neighbour's driveway.

Once a run is empty, we rinse it through with plain water toward the outlet and
watch it drain. That rinse tells us two things at once: whether the downspout is
clear, and whether the gutter is pitched so water actually reaches it. A run that
holds a puddle in the middle after rinsing is sagging or has lost its fall.

Downspouts that do not clear from the top get worked from the bottom. The
underground drains some houses have at the base of their downspouts are a
different matter; we can tell you whether water is reaching them, but we do not
excavate or jet buried lines.

## Where does the water go after it leaves the downspout?

Somewhere sensible, ideally. On Burlington's level ground the discharge point
matters more than people think. A downspout that empties straight onto the soil
beside the foundation puts the whole roof's runoff into one spot every time it
rains, and on flat ground that water has nowhere to go but down and sideways.

A few things we check while we are there:

1. Whether each downspout has an extension or splash block carrying water away
   from the wall.
2. Whether any outlet is disconnected, crushed or has pulled away from its
   bracket.
3. Whether overflow has left mud splash or staining on the siding below.

We will point out what we find. Fixing grading or drainage is not our trade,
but knowing about it early is worth a great deal to the house.

## What affects the price of a gutter clean in Burlington?

Every quote is written on site, after walking round the building, and it rests on
a handful of things:

1. **Linear footage.** The total length of gutter is the starting point.
2. **Height and storeys.** A single-storey ranch clears quickly; a two-storey
   with a steep pitch above the gutter line takes more setup.
3. **Condition.** A gutter cleaned last autumn is a light job. One that has not
   been touched in several years may hold packed sludge and growth.
4. **Access.** Planting beds, fences, decks and tight side passages between
   buildings slow the work.
5. **Building type.** Attached and multi-unit buildings often have long
   continuous runs and shared downspouts, which we price on the building rather
   than per door.

Pairing the gutters with roof cleaning or a house wash usually brings the total
down, because the ladders, the setup and the drive are shared.

## Gutters on rentals and multi-unit buildings

Burlington has an unusually large share of housing that is not a detached house:
of 4,246 units, only 2,137 are. Rental ownership is common too. That means the
person arranging a gutter clean here quite often does not live under the gutter
in question.

We make that easy. Tell us who approves the quote, who has access, and whether
tenants need notice, and we will arrange the rest. After the work you get
photographs of the cleared runs and a short note of anything that needs
attention, which is useful if you are a long way from the property or managing
several at once.

## Keeping gutter debris out of Gages Slough

Burlington Municipal Code 14.05.070(B) bars anyone from discharging pollutants,
or water carrying them, into the city's storm system. Rotting leaf sludge washed
off a driveway counts, and the destination makes it more than a technicality:
most of Burlington's runoff flows into Gages Slough and is pumped from there to
the Skagit River.

So the debris is bagged, the flush water goes down your own downspouts rather
than across the pavement, and any spill on a driveway is swept up rather than
hosed toward the kerb. The wider picture is on our page about
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).
