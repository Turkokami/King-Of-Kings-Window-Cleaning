From the street, a Burlington roof usually looks fine until it suddenly does not.
The first sign is a faint dark streak running down from the ridge, or a line of
green tufts along the lower edge where the shingles meet the gutter. By the time
the moss is visible from the pavement it has generally been growing for a few
seasons.

Cleaning it early is cheaper and gentler on the roof than cleaning it late. This
page explains what we find on roofs in this town and how we treat them.

## What grows on Burlington roofs?

Three things, usually in combination.

**Moss** forms cushions on the shaded slopes and along the lower courses, where
the roof holds moisture longest. Its roots work into the gaps between shingles,
and every clump acts like a small sponge.

**Algae** shows as the black or dark grey streaking that runs down a roof in
vertical stripes. It is mostly cosmetic, but it signals a surface that stays
damp long enough for more to follow.

**Lichen** appears as flat, crusty grey-green or pale spots, firmly attached and
slow to shift. It grips hard and is the stubbornest of the three.

Burlington gives all of them what they need. The town lies low on the Skagit
floodplain, with the river along its southern and southeastern edges, and roofs
on flat valley ground spend long stretches of the cool season damp. Any slope
that faces north or sits under a tree stays wet longest and greens up first.

## Is my roof old enough to need cleaning?

In Burlington, quite possibly. The median house here was built in 1993, and
1,517 of the town's 4,246 housing units date from 2000 or later. That puts a
large share of the town's houses somewhere between their second and fourth
decade, and their roofs with them: old enough for
growth to be well established, and young enough that removing it is still
protecting a roof with useful years ahead of it.

Age alone does not decide it, though. A newer roof in heavy shade can carry more
moss than an older one in full sun. The questions that matter more:

- Is there green along the lower edge or in the valleys?
- Are there dark vertical streaks on any slope?
- Do your gutters keep filling with granules or green debris?
- Does one face of the roof look noticeably darker than the others?

A yes to any of these is worth a look.

## What does a roof clean include?

A standard clean covers the whole roof surface and the edges that affect it:

- **Loose moss removed by hand** from the shingle surface, working with the lay
  of the shingles rather than against it
- **A low-pressure treatment** applied to every slope to kill remaining moss,
  algae and lichen
- **Ridges, valleys and the lower courses** given extra attention, since these
  hold growth longest
- **Planting and hard surfaces below the eaves** protected, wetted and rinsed
- **Gutters cleared** of the moss and debris that comes off the roof
- **Photographs** before and after, and of anything you should know about

Skylights, chimney crowns and roof-mounted solar panels are cleaned or worked
around as agreed on the quote.

## How is a roof cleaned without damaging it?

By not using pressure on it. A pressure washer will take moss off a shingle
roof, but it takes the protective granule layer with it and can drive water up
under the laps. The roof looks clean for a season and has lost part of its life.

Instead, the treatment does the work. We apply it at low pressure across the
roof, let it dwell, and let the growth die back. Loose moss is brushed or
scraped off gently by hand first so the treatment reaches the surface beneath.
Over the following weeks the dead growth dries, loosens and washes away with
ordinary rain.

How shingle roofs respond to this is covered in our profile of
[composition asphalt shingle](/surface-library/composition-asphalt-shingle/).
Painted metal roofs need a different approach again, covered under
[metal roof soft washing](/services/metal-roof-soft-washing/).

## What if the moss has already worked under the shingles?

Then removal needs more care, not more force. Moss that has rooted into the gaps
between shingles lifts their edges and lets water wick underneath. Tearing it
out aggressively can crack or break the shingle it has grown into. We loosen
what comes away freely, treat the rest, and let it release on its own time.

If we see lifted or damaged shingles, we photograph them and tell you. We are
roof cleaners, not roofers or inspectors, and a shingle that needs replacing is a
job for a roofing contractor. The pattern is explained in more detail on
[moss under shingles](/services/moss-removal/moss-under-shingles/), and ongoing
moss control is covered under [moss removal](/services/moss-removal/).

## Where does the roof runoff go?

Down the roof, into the gutters, and through the downspouts, which is exactly
why it needs managing. Wherever a downspout ties into an underground line that
could reach the street system, treatment runoff can end up in the city's
drainage. In Burlington that drainage mostly empties into Gages Slough and is
pumped on into the Skagit River.

Burlington Municipal Code 14.05.070 prohibits anything other than storm water
entering that system and names soaps, detergents and ammonia among the illicit
discharges. So on a roof job we:

1. Identify every downspout and where it discharges.
2. Divert outlets that may lead to the storm system onto lawn or planting.
3. Keep treatment off driveways and paths that shed toward the street.
4. Rinse plants below the eaves before and after the application.
5. Walk the ground at the end to be sure nothing is still running off.

The full reasoning is set out on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## What affects the cost of a roof clean in Burlington?

Every price is set on site, after seeing the roof. What moves it:

1. **Roof area**, the base of every quote.
2. **Pitch and height.** A low single-storey roof is quick to access; a steep or
   two-storey roof needs more setup and more care.
3. **How much growth there is.** A roof with light streaking is a treatment
   visit. One with thick moss along every lower course needs hand removal first.
4. **Complexity.** Valleys, dormers, skylights and chimneys all add time.
5. **Runoff control.** Downspouts that connect underground, or a driveway
   sloping to the street, need more preparation.

Booking roof and gutters together is almost always more economical than two
separate visits.

## When should a Burlington roof be cleaned?

In the dry part of the year. Burlington has no long-term weather station of its
own; the nearest, Mt Vernon 3 WNW, recorded July as its driest month over
1956-2005, averaging 1.16 inches. A treatment applied then has time to dry onto
the growth and work before rain returns.

Late spring and early autumn are workable on a settled spell. What we avoid is
treating a roof ahead of a forecast downpour, which rinses the treatment off
before it has done its job, and working on a roof slick with frost or rain,
which is a safety matter first.

Once clean, most roofs here go several years before needing another full
treatment, and the shaded slopes set the pace. A light maintenance treatment
partway through that interval, applied before growth becomes visible again, is
usually quicker and cheaper than waiting for a full return of moss, and it is
the simplest way to keep the lower courses clear.

## Cleaning roofs on rentals and shared buildings

Burlington has more renters than owner-occupiers, and only about half its
housing is detached houses, so a roof here often belongs to someone who seldom
sees it. That tends to be the roof that goes longest between cleans, because
nobody living underneath is looking up at it.

For a landlord or manager the practical questions are access and notice. We can
arrange the visit through a tenant, keep cars and paths clear of the work area,
and send photographs of every slope before and after, so an owner who lives
elsewhere can see what was done without driving over. On attached buildings
with a shared roof, tell us who approves the work for the whole structure; a
roof treated on one unit and not the next simply reseeds itself.

A roof clean between tenancies also fits neatly with the other work a turnover
usually brings, since the ladders are already up.

## Why do roof cleaning and gutter cleaning go together?

Because one feeds the other. A mossy roof sheds into the gutters all year, and
a roof treatment loosens growth that ends up in the gutters over the following
weeks. Clearing the gutters as part of the roof job, and again in autumn, keeps
the whole system flowing. The gutter side of that is covered on
[gutter cleaning in Burlington](/locations/burlington/gutter-cleaning/).
