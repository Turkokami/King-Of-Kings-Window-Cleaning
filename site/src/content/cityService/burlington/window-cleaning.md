A Burlington window gets dirty from three directions. Dust drifts in off the
fields in the dry months. Traffic film builds on the side of the house that
faces a busy road. And through the long wet season, rain dries on the glass in
spots and runs, leaving a pattern you only notice when low sun hits it.

We clean all of it off, inside and out. The other thing that shapes window work
in this town is who owns the glass, and that is covered further down.

## What makes windows in Burlington dirty?

The setting, mostly. Burlington sits in the middle of working farmland: Skagit
County growers cultivate about 90,000 acres and more than 90 crops, and the soil
that tilling and harvest lift into the air comes down on glass in a fine, even
layer. After an overnight dew it bonds to the pane as a dull film that a hose
will not shift.

Then there is traffic. I-5 runs through the town, and a long stretch of
commercial development grew up along Burlington Boulevard after the Cascade
Mall opened in 1989. Homes near those corridors pick up a grey, slightly greasy
road film, heaviest on glass facing the traffic.

Rain adds the third layer. Water that runs down a dusty window picks up the
dirt, then dries as streaks and droplet marks. On a north-facing window that
stays damp for days, green growth can start in the corners of the frame.

## Why does it matter that so many Burlington homes are rented?

Because it changes when and how windows get cleaned. More homes here are rented
than owned: 2,178 of the 3,925 occupied homes. And only 2,137 of the town's 4,246
housing units are detached houses, which leaves roughly half in attached and
multi-unit buildings.

For windows, that means a lot of the work is arranged by someone who does not
live behind the glass. The common requests we plan for:

- **Turnover cleans** timed between one tenant moving out and the next moving in
- **Exterior-only visits** on occupied units, where going indoors is not
  practical
- **Whole-building cleans** on duplexes and small multi-unit properties
- **Photographs sent to an owner** who lives elsewhere and wants to see the
  finished result

Clean glass does more than almost anything else to make an empty unit look
cared for in listing photographs, because it changes the light in every room.

## What is included in a window clean?

The standard residential clean covers:

- **Both faces of every accessible pane**, interior and exterior
- **Screens** taken out, washed, dried and returned to their own frames
- **Sills and tracks** vacuumed and wiped, since grit in a track wears the seals
  every time the sash slides
- **Frames** wiped down where the material allows
- **Cobwebs** cleared from around the openings
- **A final look together** before we leave, in daylight

Skylights, glass railings, storm windows and conservatory roofs are priced
separately because access varies so much. Post-construction glass, with paint,
mortar and label adhesive on it, is quoted as its own job.

## How is the glass actually cleaned?

With two methods, chosen window by window.

**Reachable glass** is cleaned by hand: a cleaning solution applied with a
scrubber to break the film, then a squeegee to take it off in one clean pull,
with the edges detailed. Physical contact is the fastest way through bonded field
dust.

**Upper floors and awkward reaches** are cleaned with a water-fed pole from the
ground. The pole carries purified water through a soft brush; the brush
loosens the dirt, and the water, having had its minerals removed, dries
without leaving marks. No ladder goes into your planting beds or onto a deck.

Screens get their own attention. Each one comes out, is washed and rinsed, dries
in the air while we work on the glass, and goes back into the frame it came
from. A screen full of summer field dust will put some of that dust straight
back on a freshly cleaned pane the first time the wind blows, so skipping them
is a false saving.

We bring our own purified water rather than filling from an outside tap.
Burlington's supply comes from Skagit PUD's Judy Reservoir system, fed by four
Cultus Mountain streams. There is no published hardness figure for it that we
could verify, and we do not need one: filtered water off our own system dries
clean whatever comes out of the tap.

## What about fogged double glazing?

That is a failed seal, not dirt. When moisture appears between the two panes of
a sealed unit, the seal around its edge has broken and damp air has got inside.
No cleaning of the outside faces can reach it; the fix is a glazing repair or a
replacement unit.

We will point these out on the quote and leave them off the bill. If you want to
understand how these units work and why they fail, our profile of
[insulated glass units](/surface-library/insulated-glass-units/) covers it.
Spotting the difference early can matter on a rental, where a failed unit is
easier to deal with between tenancies than during one.

## How long does a window clean take?

Less time than most people expect for a small home, and more than they expect
for a big one. A single-storey house or a ground-floor unit with a modest number
of openings is usually finished within a morning. A two-storey house with large
fixed panes, a stairwell window or a lot of glass over a deck can take most of a
day, because the reach is harder and each pane is bigger.

Screens and tracks add real time. A home with every screen in place and tracks
full of summer dust takes noticeably longer than one where the screens were
stored for winter, and we build that into the booking rather than rushing it.

For a rental between tenants, the unit being empty helps: no furniture to move
away from the glass, no one working from home at the window we need, and no
need to plan around a household's day. If you can give us a clear window of
time on move-out, we can usually fit the clean inside it.

## What affects the price of a window clean here?

Every quote is written on site after a walk round. The main drivers:

1. **Number of panes**, the starting point.
2. **Size of the glass.** Big picture windows take longer than their count
   suggests.
3. **Access.** Ground-floor glass on open lawn is quick; upper floors over a
   deck, a roof or thick planting take more time.
4. **Condition.** Glass cleaned last season is maintenance; glass carrying
   several years of field dust and road film is a restoration clean the first
   time and lighter work after.
5. **Inside, outside or both**, and extras such as screens, tracks and skylights.
6. **Occupancy.** Working around furniture and tenants takes longer than an
   empty unit between leases.

The second clean nearly always costs less than the first, because we are
keeping the glass clear rather than rescuing it.

## When should Burlington windows be cleaned?

Two times a year work for most households.

**Spring** clears the grime of a long wet season: rain spotting, frame-corner
growth, and the film from months of damp. With July the driest month at the
nearest long-term weather station, in Mount Vernon, the glass then stays clear
into the summer.

**Early autumn**, once the dry-season dust has settled and before the rain sets
in, is the second. Clean too early in the summer and the field dust puts the
film straight back.

**On demand** covers the rest: a tenant changeover, a house going on the market,
or the end of a renovation. None of these follow the calendar.

We avoid a hard frost, when water freezes on the glass before it can be worked.

## What happens to the water from a window clean?

Very little of it goes anywhere. A hand clean uses a bucket of solution and a
squeegee, and the volume that reaches the ground is small. The water-fed pole
uses purified water with nothing added.

Even so, Burlington's rules are clear. The city's code lists soaps and
detergents among illicit discharges to its storm system, and most of the town's
runoff flows into Gages Slough and on to the Skagit River. So solution buckets
are emptied into a sink or onto soil, never into a street drain, and the pole
work, which uses plain water, is kept to the property wherever possible.

## Do you clean shop windows in Burlington?

Yes. Burlington has a large commercial side, much of it along Burlington
Boulevard, and storefront glass runs to a different rhythm from a house: often
weekly or monthly, timed around opening hours, with the entrance doors taking
the most wear. That work is covered under
[commercial window cleaning](/services/commercial-window-cleaning/), and we
quote the frequency along with the first visit.

## What goes well with a window clean?

Windows should be the last job on the house. If the siding is due a wash, that
comes first, because it wets and splashes the glass; see
[house washing in Burlington](/locations/burlington/house-washing/). If the
gutters are overflowing, water running down the face of the house will streak
fresh glass within a week, so clearing them first protects the result.
