A Burlington house gets dirty in a quiet, even way. Nothing splashes it; it
simply collects, a little more each season, until one bright afternoon the
north wall looks green and the side facing the road looks grey, and the white
trim is not quite white any more.

A soft wash takes all of that off without harming the siding. What makes the
job particular to this town is the water: where the rinse ends up, and what the
city's code says it may and may not carry.

## What builds up on siding in Burlington?

Three kinds of soiling, often on the same house.

**Green growth on the damp side.** Burlington lies on low, level ground where the
Skagit River swings southwest across its floodplain, and walls that face away
from the sun stay wet for long stretches between autumn and spring. Algae finds
those walls first, starting at the lowest courses and under the eaves.

**Fine dust from the farmland.** Skagit County farmers work about 90,000 acres
and grow upward of 90 crops. Cultivation and harvest put fine soil into the air
in the drier months, and it settles on siding, sills and trim, where the next
rain turns it into a thin muddy film.

**Road grime near the corridors.** Houses within sight of I-5 or the busier roads
around Burlington Boulevard pick up a grey, slightly oily traffic film, heaviest
on the elevation that faces the road.

The mix varies from street to street, which is why we look at each wall on the
quote rather than pricing a house from its footprint.

## Why is one side of the house always greener?

Because that side never fully dries. A wall that gets no direct sun from late
October into spring can stay damp for weeks, and algae and mildew need little
more than steady moisture and something to grip. Siding around downspouts and
under overflowing gutters is the other favourite spot, for the same reason.

On most siding this growth is cosmetic and comes off cleanly. The catch is that
it returns sooner on a wall that stays shaded, so a north elevation may want
washing more often than the rest of the house. We cover the pattern in more
detail on [north-facing wall mildew](/services/house-washing/north-facing-wall-mildew/).

If the green is concentrated under one stretch of gutter, the gutter is part of
the answer too, and a wash alone will only buy a season.

## What does a house wash include?

A standard wash covers the whole exterior envelope from the ground to the eaves:

- **Siding on every elevation**, including the walls behind shrubs where access
  allows
- **Soffits and eave undersides**, where cobwebs and dust collect
- **Fascia boards and the outer face of gutters** rinsed as part of the pass
- **Window and door trim**, sills and frames
- **Exterior light fittings, vents and the house number plate** cleaned by hand
  where they sit within the wash area
- **Pre-wetting and after-rinsing of planting** along the foundation

Heavily streaked gutter faces need their own treatment, called
[gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/), and are
quoted as an add-on. Decks, fences, patios and driveways are separate jobs.

## How is a Burlington house actually washed?

Softly. We apply a cleaning solution at low pressure, let it sit long enough to
break down the growth and loosen the film, then rinse at low pressure from the
top down. The solution does the work; the water only carries it away.

This matters because the materials on a house are not built to take a pressure
washer. High pressure can force water up behind lap siding, lift paint from
trim, etch softer surfaces and blow out window seals. Low pressure removes
algae and road grime just as completely, and it leaves the surface intact. The
approach on one widely used cladding is set out in our
[vinyl siding](/surface-library/vinyl-siding/) profile.

We mix solution strength to the problem in front of us, not to a fixed recipe.
A lightly dusty wall needs very little. A north elevation carrying a few seasons
of growth needs more, and more dwell time, which is one reason we would rather
wash on a mild, overcast day than in hot sun that dries the solution before it
has done its job.

## What does Burlington's code say about washing a house?

More than most homeowners would guess. Burlington Municipal Code 14.05.070(B)
bars discharging into the city's storm system any pollutant, or any water
carrying one, other than storm water. Subsection (C) spells out what counts,
and its list names soaps, detergents and ammonia, and steam-cleaning wastes.

There is a narrow allowance in 14.05.070(E)(4): routine external building
wash-down is permitted, but only if it does not use detergents. A plain-water
rinse of a wall sits inside that allowance. A wash that uses a cleaning
solution does not, so its runoff must be kept away from the storm system
altogether.

That is the working rule we follow on every Burlington house, whatever the
size of the job.

## Where does the rinse water go?

On to your property and into the ground, not down the street. It matters here
because most of Burlington's stormwater flows into Gages Slough, the waterway
the Tammi Wilson Memorial Trail runs beside, and is pumped from there into the
Skagit River. The city lists cleaning up Gages Slough as a very high priority.

Before we start, we plan the path the water will take:

1. **Find every storm inlet** within reach of the runoff, on the driveway, the
   street edge and any yard drains that connect to the city system.
2. **Cover or block those inlets** for the duration of the wash.
3. **Direct the rinse onto lawn and planting beds**, where the soil absorbs and
   breaks it down, rather than onto hard surfaces that shed it.
4. **Use the least solution the wall needs**, and plain water wherever the
   soiling allows it.
5. **Check the ground before we leave**, so nothing is still running toward the
   kerb.

The full reasoning, with the sources, is on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## What affects the cost of a house wash here?

Every figure is quoted on site, after a walk round the building. The main
drivers are:

1. **Size and height.** Wall area sets the base; a second storey adds setup and
   reach.
2. **Condition.** A house washed within the last few years needs a light pass.
   One with heavy growth on a shaded wall needs stronger mix and more dwell time.
3. **Containment.** A house with lawn on all sides is simple. One with a
   driveway sloping toward a street drain, or a paved yard with its own inlets,
   takes more preparation.
4. **Access.** Tight side passages, fences built against the wall and mature
   foundation planting all slow the work.
5. **Add-ons**, such as gutter brightening or a window clean on the same visit.

A word on comparing quotes. A lower figure sometimes means the rinse plan has
been left out: no inlets covered, solution sprayed straight off the driveway,
and the wash finished in half the time. On a Burlington street that shortcut
sends detergent toward Gages Slough, the very discharge the city code names. Ask
any company you are comparing how they intend to handle the runoff, and weigh
the answer alongside the price.

Repeat washes cost less than the first one. Once the heavy growth is gone, a
maintenance wash every couple of years is lighter work, uses less solution and
takes less time on site.

## What time of year suits a house wash in Burlington?

Summer, for most homes. The nearest long-term station to Burlington is in
Mount Vernon, since the town has none of its own, and its record puts July as
the driest month, at just 1.16 inches on average. Dry weather matters for two
reasons: the solution is not diluted by a passing shower before it works, and
the walls dry out afterwards instead of staying damp for the growth to return.

Late spring and early autumn are good second choices on a dry spell. Winter
washing is possible on a calm, dry day, but it is harder to schedule and the
shaded side often stays wet anyway.

## Washing rentals and multi-unit buildings

In Burlington, more homes are rented than owned: 2,178 of the 3,925 occupied
homes, by the most recent census estimates. A tired exterior costs a landlord
more than it costs an owner-occupier, because it is the first thing a
prospective tenant sees and the last thing anyone thinks to fix.

For rentals we can arrange access through the tenant or a key-holder, work
around parked cars and shared paths, and send before and after photographs to
an owner who is not on site. Attached and multi-unit buildings are quoted as a
single building, with the containment plan drawn for the whole site rather than
each door.

## What goes well with a house wash?

The wash is often the middle of a bigger job. If the roof is carrying moss, it
should be treated first, because runoff from the roof streaks freshly washed
walls. If the gutters are overflowing, clearing them stops the next streak
before it starts. And windows go last: the wash wets the glass but does not
clean it, so booking [window cleaning in Burlington](/locations/burlington/window-cleaning/)
straight afterwards finishes the house properly.
