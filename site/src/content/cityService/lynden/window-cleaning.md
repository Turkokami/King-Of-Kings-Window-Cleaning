Most window trouble in Lynden is not dramatic. It is a slow build of rain splash,
pollen, film and cobweb that turns a clear pane grey over a season, plus the
tracks and sills that quietly fill with grit. Clean glass changes how a room feels
more than almost any other maintenance job, and it is one of the few that shows
from the inside of the house as much as from the street.

We clean the inside and the outside, take the screens out and wash them, and
clear the tracks and sills. The method depends on how high the window is and
what is below it, and the water we use comes with us rather than out of your tap.

## What makes Lynden windows dirty?

A mix of weather, setting and ordinary living. Rain is the biggest single
contributor. The long-record weather station nearest town, at Clearbrook, averages
46.71 inches of precipitation a year, and every shower that hits a dusty pane
dries into spots and runs. Splash from beds and paths spatters the lower panes of
ground-floor windows, much as it does the bottom boards of the siding.

Setting adds its own layer. Lynden's economy moved from logging to agriculture, dairy
and berry farming especially, around the turn of the twentieth century, and glass that
faces working land can pick up a fine dry film after dry spells that looks more
like haze than dirt. Glass under trees gets pollen, sap mist and organic film in
the frame corners.

Inside, the film comes from the house itself: cooking, heating, candles, pets and
winter condensation. It builds slowly, which is why people are often surprised
by how much of the grey was on the inside.

## Does Lynden have hard water?

Nobody can honestly tell you from the published record. Lynden draws its drinking
water from the Nooksack River and treats it at the city's own plant. Neither the
2021 nor the 2024 water quality report gives a hardness figure, so there is no
city number to point to, and we will not invent one to sell a treatment.

What is true everywhere is that tap water dried on glass leaves its dissolved
content behind. Sprinkler overspray, a hose rinse left to dry in the sun, and
runoff from a stained sill can all leave marks, whatever the water's source. Where
we find mineral spotting on a Lynden window, we treat it as what it is on that
pane, not as a fact about the town. For our own rinse on upper storeys we use
purified water we bring with us, so our work adds nothing to the glass as it
dries.

## What is included in a window clean?

A standard residential booking covers:

- **Outside and inside glass** on every window we can safely reach
- **Screens** taken out, washed, dried and returned to the opening they came from
- **Tracks** vacuumed and wiped, with grit removed from the corners
- **Sills and frames** wiped where they are washable
- **Cobwebs** cleared from around each opening and the eave just above it
- **A final look round** with you before we leave, in daylight

Skylights, glass railings, storm windows and conservatory roofs are quoted
separately because access varies so much from house to house. Paint, silicone
and construction residue on glass is also its own job, since removing it without
scratching the pane needs a different approach from a maintenance clean.

## How is the glass actually cleaned?

Two ways, chosen window by window.

**Glass we can reach from the ground or a short ladder** gets washed by hand. A
scrubber loosens the film with a mild cleaning solution, and a squeegee takes it
off in a controlled pass, with the edges detailed by cloth. The solution is used
sparingly and comes off on the squeegee and towel rather than being hosed down the
wall.

**Upper storeys and anything above an obstacle** get a water-fed pole. A brush on
the end scrubs the glass while purified water flows through it, and because that
water carries nothing dissolved in it, the pane is rinsed and left to dry on its
own without spotting. It lets us clean a high gable window, or a pane
above a deck or a conservatory, without a ladder touching the house.

Modern double-glazed units need a little care in both methods, mostly around the
seals and any coatings, which is covered in our [insulated glass units
profile](/surface-library/insulated-glass-units/).

## Do newer Lynden houses need anything different?

Often they have larger glass, and larger glass behaves differently. Lynden's
housing is young, with about 2,773 of its 6,210 units built in 2000 or later and
325 of those since 2020. Houses of that era tend to carry bigger fixed panes,
taller gable windows and more glass on the upper floor than older designs did.

Big panes show every streak, so they take more time per window even if the window
count is low. Tall gable and stairwell glass is exactly what the pole is for. And
a house that is only a year or two old may still have builder's residue on the
glass, such as labels, paint flecks or mortar spatter. That is a
post-construction clean, priced separately, because careless scraping can scratch
new glass for good.

## What about Lynden's older homes?

They need a slower hand. The census counts 483 Lynden homes built in 1939 or
earlier and 410 from the 1950s. Older houses are more likely to have wooden
sashes, painted frames, putty-glazed panes and storm windows, and every one of
those is less forgiving than a modern vinyl frame.

On an older window we check the putty and paint before scrubbing, keep water
away from bare wood, and handle sashes and storms carefully. If a window will
not open, or a frame is soft, we tell you rather than force it.

## What drives the price here?

Each quote is built on site from a handful of things:

1. **The number of windows**, which sets the starting point
2. **Pane size**, since one big picture window can take longer than several small ones
3. **Height and access**, including decks, slopes and planting under the glass
4. **Inside, outside or both**, with inside work taking longer per window
5. **Condition**, because a first clean after years of neglect takes more than a routine visit
6. **Extras**, from skylights and storm windows to post-construction residue

A regular customer on a steady schedule usually pays less per visit than someone
booking a one-off restoration clean, simply because there is less to remove.

## When should I book?

Spring is the most popular time, clearing off the winter's rain spotting and
interior film once the days lengthen. Late summer or early autumn is the other
natural window, after the dry months have left their dust and before the
November and December rain begins. The local record shows those two months as
the wettest of the year, averaging over six inches each at Clearbrook.

A clean before a house goes on the market is always worth it, because the
listing photos are taken from the inside looking out. We avoid cleaning glass
at or below freezing, since water left on the pane can ice and the finish suffers.

## Where does the rinse water go?

Mostly onto the ground right under the window, and in very small volumes.
Lynden's code, at 13.24.160(C)(4), permits routine external building wash-down
without detergent as a conditional discharge, and the purified water from a pole
contains nothing at all. Hand-washed glass uses a small amount of solution that
comes away on the squeegee and cloth.

On larger glass jobs over paving, we still watch where water runs, because the
city's storm drains feed Fishtrap Creek. How we approach runoff across all our
services is on our [wash water and storm drain
page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Can windows be combined with other work?

Yes, and they should go last. If the siding is being washed, it rinses down over
the glass, so book the windows to follow on the same visit; the [Lynden house

for roof and gutter work, which can drop grit onto glass. The general detail

## How should I get the house ready?

Very little is needed, but a few minutes beforehand speeds the day up and keeps
your things safe:

- Clear ornaments, plants and photos off the inside sills of the windows being
  cleaned
- Unlock any windows you want cleaned on both sides, and tell us about any that
  stick or should not be opened
- Move cars away from the walls if upper glass is directly above the driveway
- Let us know about pets, alarm sensors on frames, and gates that must stay shut
- Point out any window you already know has a cracked pane, failed seal or loose
  screen, so it is noted before anyone touches it

Interior work means we will be inside for part of the visit, so tell us about
rooms that are off limits or floors you would like covered. Outside, nothing
needs moving unless furniture sits directly against the glass.
