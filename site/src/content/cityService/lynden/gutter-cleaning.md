A gutter in Lynden has one job that matters above the rest: get the November and
December rain off the roof and away from the house without spilling it down the
wall. Whether it manages that depends almost entirely on what is sitting in the
trough when the heavy weather arrives.

We clear the troughs by hand, bag what comes out, flush each downspout, and leave
nothing behind that the next rain could carry to a street drain. The reason for
that last part is local, and it is written into the city code.

## What fills gutters on a Lynden house?

It depends on what stands near the house, and Lynden has real trees in places:
Lynden City Park sits among tall conifers on Fishtrap Creek, and a lot next to a
stand of mature trees collects needles and cones year round. A house on open
ground still fills its gutters, just with different material.

What we typically pull from a trough, in roughly the order it builds up:

- **Shingle grit**, the fine mineral granules that wash off composition roofing
  and settle into a sludge at the low end of each run
- **Moss fragments** that break off the roof in wet weather and root again in
  the silt, sometimes growing right in the trough
- **Leaves, needles and seed**, wherever trees stand within reach of the eaves,
  including trees in a neighbour's yard
- **Windblown material** such as dust, pollen and plant litter that sticks to
  the damp silt and thickens it
- **Nests and the odd lost ball**, usually at a corner or right above a downspout

Once a layer of that silt forms, it holds water between storms, and a trough that
stays damp becomes a planter. If moss on the roof is shedding into the gutters,
the lasting fix starts above them, on the [Lynden roof cleaning
page](/locations/lynden/roof-cleaning/).

## Why does the rain calendar set the gutter calendar?

Because the heaviest water arrives all at once. The Clearbrook cooperative
station, the long-record weather station nearest Lynden, averages 6.17 inches of
precipitation in November and 6.22 in December, set against 1.47 in July. It sits
in the area rather than inside the town, so read it as the regional pattern, but
the pattern is clear enough to plan around.

A gutter that is half blocked copes with a July shower and fails the first
sustained November storm. That is why an autumn clearing, finished before the
turn into November, does far more good than the same clearing in spring. Spring
still has a place when trees overhang the roof, because material that fell over
the winter has usually packed down by then.

## What does a gutter clean include?

Every accessible run on the house, done the same way each time:

1. Debris taken out of the troughs by hand and scoop, not blown off the roof onto
   your lawn, beds or car
2. Everything bagged and removed from the property
3. Each downspout flushed from the top until water runs clear out of the bottom
4. Troughs rinsed so the fall of each run can be seen and checked
5. A walk round with you afterward, pointing out anything that needs a repairer

Detached garages and outbuildings are included when you ask for them on the
quote. Gutter guards, where a house already has them, change the job
considerably, so mention them when you book and we will price accordingly.

## How is the work actually done?

From ladders set with care, standing clear of the trough itself, which bends and
lets go of its hangers if anyone leans a ladder on it. On single-storey runs we
work along the eave from the ground up; on two-storey runs, the reach and the
ladder footing on soft lawn decide the approach, and some sections are handled
from the roof where the pitch allows it safely.

Hand clearing is slower than blowing a gutter out, and slower is the point. A
blower throws the wet silt across the siding, the windows and the lawn, and the
fine part of it ends up where the next rain rinses it toward a drain. Taking it
out by hand keeps it in a bag.

The downspout flush is where the job is actually proven. We run water in at the
top of each one and watch it leave at the bottom. If it backs up, we clear the
blockage from above where we can reach it. If a drop runs into buried pipe we
cannot clear, we say so plainly rather than call the job done.

## Where does the debris go, and why does it matter here?

It goes into a bag and leaves with us, because Lynden's municipal code does not
let it go anywhere else. Chapter 13.24 lists illicit discharges to the city storm
system, and item 24 on that list is collected leaves or branches. Rinsing gutter
sludge down the driveway, or sweeping it to the kerb for the rain to take, puts
exactly that material into the system.

The city's stormwater page explains where it would end up: most of what enters
Lynden's storm drains flows into Fishtrap Creek and then the Nooksack River. The
same list names soaps and detergents too, which is why the water we use to flush
downspouts carries no additive at all. Our [wash water and storm drain
page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/) covers how we handle runoff on
every kind of job.

## What drives the price on a Lynden house?

There is no flat rate, because houses differ too much for one. The quote comes
down to:

- **Length of run**, measured along the eaves, including any garage or shop
- **Height**, since a two-storey eave takes longer and needs more ladder work than
  a single-storey one
- **Access**, meaning soft lawn, steep slopes, decks and planting under the eaves
- **Condition**, where a gutter cleared last autumn is quick and one left for
  several years may hold packed silt and live moss
- **Downspout count**, and whether any drop runs underground
- **Extras** such as brightening the outer faces or clearing an outbuilding

A second visit usually costs less than the first, because we are maintaining
rather than excavating.

## What problems start in a blocked gutter?

Most of them show up somewhere other than the gutter. Water that cannot get down
the downspout spills over the front edge and falls straight onto the ground at
the foundation, or runs behind the trough and down the fascia. Over a season or
two that means rot in the fascia board, staining on the siding, soil washed out
of beds and water pooling where it should never collect. The signs and causes are
laid out on the [overflowing gutters page](/services/gutter-cleaning/overflowing-gutters/).

The black vertical streaks that appear on the outer face of many troughs are a
different issue, a film that clearing the inside will not touch. That is dealt
with by [gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/), which
we quote separately.

## What do snow and ice do to Lynden gutters?

They add weight at the worst moment. The Clearbrook station averages 16 inches of
snow a year, mostly in December and January, which puts the heaviest snow load on
the gutters straight after the wettest months. A trough still holding wet silt
freezes solid, and meltwater backed up behind that ice has nowhere to go but over
the edge or under the first course of shingles.

We do not work on eaves carrying snow or ice, since the footing is unsafe and the
trough is at its most fragile. Once it has cleared, a late-winter check is
worthwhile on any house where the gutters were full going into December, because
that is when sagging runs and pulled hangers tend to show.

## How often should a Lynden gutter be cleared?

Once a year for a house with open sky above it, timed for October or the first
days of November. Twice a year where trees stand near the roof, with the second
visit in spring. Houses with moss on the roof sometimes need more until the roof
itself is dealt with, because the moss keeps feeding the troughs. The general
background on frequency and method sits on our main [gutter cleaning
service page](/services/gutter-cleaning/).

## What can I check from the ground between visits?

Quite a lot, and none of it needs a ladder. The most useful time to look is during
a proper downpour, standing somewhere dry with a view of the eaves.

Water sheeting over the front lip of a trough, especially at a corner or midway
along a long run, means a blockage or a run that has lost its fall. A downspout
that stays dry while the gutter above it is overflowing is packed somewhere along
its length. Plants sprouting along the roof edge tell you there is soil in the
trough, and soil means it has been a while. Splash marks, fresh staining or
washed-out mulch directly below the eaves show where water has been landing
instead of being carried away.

After a cold snap, look along the line of each gutter from the end of the house.
A run that dips between hangers has usually carried ice or wet silt heavier than
it was built for. None of these signs means you need us tomorrow, but each one is
worth mentioning when you ring, because it tells us where to look first.
