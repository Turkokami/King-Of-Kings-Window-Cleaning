A Bellingham gutter is less a drain than a catch tray for whatever grows above
it. Under Douglas-fir it fills in every month of the year, and it fills fastest
in exactly the season when the rain has nowhere else to go.

Clearing one properly takes more than a scoop and a glance. The trough has to be
emptied by hand, the outlet opened up, and water run through to the bottom of
each downspout to prove the whole path works. That is the job we do, and the
rest of this page is what it involves here.

## Why do Bellingham gutters fill so quickly?

Because the city is built under a forest. Roughly 40% of Bellingham sits under
tree canopy, 7,252 acres of it, and the City's draft urban forest plan is
working toward 45% by 2050. Whatever the canopy sheds, a gutter catches.

The mix matters as much as the amount. The forest species here are western
redcedar, Douglas-fir, big-leaf maple, vine maple, red alder, black cottonwood
and Sitka spruce, and along the streets it is mainly maples, cherries and oaks.
Conifers drop needles and fine litter steadily, all year. The broadleaf trees
drop in a heap each autumn. A house with both near it gets a constant trickle
and then a flood, and the flood tends to arrive just as the rain begins in
earnest.

Big-leaf maple deserves its own mention, because its leaves are large enough to
lie flat over a downspout outlet and seal it. One leaf in the right place can
back up an entire run.

## What do wet fir needles do inside a gutter?

They knit together. Dry needles lie loose and will sometimes wash through on
their own. Wet ones, which is what Bellingham gutters hold from October on, lock
into a dense, spongy mat that holds water, slows the flow to a seep and builds
up against the outlet. Left for a season it breaks down into a black silt that
sets like mud at the bottom of the trough and feeds whatever takes root in it.

That mat is also heavy. A run packed with waterlogged needles carries far more
weight than it was hung to carry, and the hangers and fascia take the strain.

and debris lifted out by hand, then every run checked through to the downspout.

## What does a gutter visit include?

The standard visit covers:

- **Every run cleared by hand**, including the short sections over porches, bay
  windows and dormers that are easy to forget
- **Debris bagged as it comes out** rather than dropped onto your planting beds
- **Outlets opened** so the downspout entry is clear, not just the trough
- **Each downspout flushed** until water runs freely at the bottom
- **Loose litter picked off the roof edge** just above the gutter, so the next
  rain does not wash it straight back in
- **A note of anything we see**, such as a sagging run, a separated seam or
  staining that points to overflow, with photos if you want them

Not included: repairs, guard installation, or clearing buried drain lines where a
downspout runs underground. Where a downspout feeds a buried pipe we confirm
water is entering it, and we tell you if it is not.

## How do we clear the gutters?

By hand, working each run toward its outlet, with the material scooped and bagged
as it comes. We do not use a blower on wet debris. It does not shift a packed
needle mat; it scatters the loose top layer over the roof, the windows and the
garden and leaves the compacted part exactly where it was.

Once a run is empty, water goes in at the high end. Watching that water travel
the length of the trough and come out at ground level is the only honest test
that the job is finished. A run that ponds, a joint that drips or a downspout
that backs up shows itself in the first minute, and it is far better found then
than during the first storm of November.

On ladders, the wet season adds its own care: soft lawns, mossy paths and slick
roof edges. Placement is chosen for the ground on the day, and where a run
cannot be reached safely we say so on the quote rather than improvising.

## What sets the cost of gutter cleaning in Bellingham?

Every quote is for the house in front of us, and it turns on a handful of
things:

1. **Length of run and number of downspouts.** More corners and outlets mean
   more to clear and more to test.
2. **Height.** Single-storey eaves are quick; a three-storey townhome or a
   hillside house with a tall downhill face is not.
3. **What is overhead.** A roof under mature conifers holds far more material
   than one on an open lot.
4. **Time since the last clean.** Loose needles come out fast; silt that has sat
   a season or two is slow, heavy work.
5. **What is below the gutter.** Decks, conservatories, steep slopes and planting
   beds all slow ladder work.
6. **What else is booked.** Gutters done on the same visit as roof or moss work
   share the setup, and the roof work sends debris into the gutter anyway.

## When should Bellingham gutters be cleaned?

Before the wet season, and again after the leaves are down. Roughly 80% of
Bellingham's 36 inches of annual rain falls between October and March, so late
September into October is the visit that matters most: the gutters go into six
wet months empty.

The catch is that the broadleaf drop often comes after that first visit. On a
lot near big-leaf maple, alder, cottonwood or a row of street maples, a second
clear once the trees are bare stops the autumn load from sitting in the trough
all winter. Under conifers, where the litter never stops, a late-winter or
spring visit keeps the mat from building into silt.

A strong southerly front can strip a canopy in an afternoon, so the calendar is
only a guide. What is actually overhead decides the schedule.

## Where does the water go?

Gutter flushing is plain water, and we keep it that way inside the trough. The
City of Bellingham does not allow wash water into a storm drain; under BMC 15.42
it goes to sanitary sewer or a vegetated area. That rule comes into play the
moment a cleaning solution is involved, which is why exterior work such as
[gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/) is rinsed
toward lawn or planting and never toward the street.

Lake Whatcom properties carry an extra layer. The lake is the city's drinking
water, and Basin One of the watershed has a zero-phosphorus rule on fertilisers
and soil amendments. Rotted gutter silt is rich organic matter, and on a
watershed property we bag and remove it rather than spread it on beds that drain
toward the lake. The full picture of Bellingham's wash-water rules is on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Older houses and taller buildings

Two kinds of Bellingham building make gutter work slower, for different reasons.

The first is the old house. Bellingham has eight National Register historic
districts, and in places like York, substantially built out by the Great
Depression, the gutter has usually been replaced at least once while the fascia
behind it has seen a century of weather. We keep ladders off runs that flex,
work gently around old hangers, and point out anything that looks tired. That is
a note from the person who has just been along the whole run, not an inspection.

The second is the new multi-family building. About three-quarters of recent
Bellingham permits were multi-family, and three-storey townhome rows put gutters
well above a standard ladder, often over parking bays and shared paths. Those
jobs are scheduled with the manager or association, with residents told in
advance.

## What if the gutters are not the whole problem?

Sometimes clearing the trough does not stop the overflow. Undersized downspouts,
a run that has lost its fall, or roof water that shoots past the gutter lip
during heavy rain all look the same from the driveway. The patterns and what
causes each are laid out under
[overflowing gutters](/services/gutter-cleaning/overflowing-gutters/).

The roof above matters too. Moss on a shaded Bellingham roof sheds clumps and
granule grit into the gutter every time it rains, which refills a clean trough
faster than the trees do. If the roof is green, clearing the gutters treats the
symptom, and [roof cleaning](/locations/bellingham/roof-cleaning/) done first
deals with the source.
