More people search for window cleaning in Bellingham than for every other
exterior-cleaning service in this city combined, and most of them are asking a
narrower question than the phrase suggests: can someone get the north side and
the upstairs done properly, and will the result last past the next front.

## Why Bellingham glass behaves the way it does

The wet season here runs October into May and delivers roughly 80% of the year's
rain. That single fact drives most of what turns up on glass.

**North and east elevations never dry.** They get no direct sun for months, so
organic film establishes in the frame corners and along the lower rail and stays
there. This is the elevation people describe as "looking dirty again a week after
it was cleaned," and it is aspect rather than workmanship.

**Interior condensation runs all winter.** Warm indoor air meeting the coldest
surface in the room leaves a film that builds cumulatively from November. It is
why exterior-only is a false economy in this city specifically.

**Tree cover does the rest.** Bellingham is a wooded town. Conifer debris, sap and
the fine organic matter that comes off a canopy in wind all land on glass, and
under a mature fir they land continuously rather than seasonally.

What Bellingham largely does *not* have, compared to the valley, is heavy mineral
spotting on municipal supply — that is more a well-water problem, and it turns up
here mostly where a sprinkler head is hitting the glass daily.

## Which side of the house gets dirtiest?

Both the north and the south, for opposite reasons. The north side collects
growth because it never dries; the south side collects spatter because it takes
the weather head-on. Prevailing wind in Bellingham is southerly in eleven months
of the year, so fronts arrive from the south and throw rain straight at that
face. Every squall leaves droplets carrying whatever was in the air, and when
they dry on the pane you get the freckled look that shows worst in low winter
sun.

Trees add their own layer. Street maples, cherries and oaks shed blossom, pollen
and leaf fragments that stick to wet glass and set hard once the first warm week
of spring dries them. Under conifers the load is steadier and finer, a grey dust
of needle litter that turns to smear the moment it rains. The damp north walls
that grow [mildew on siding](/services/house-washing/north-facing-wall-mildew/)
are the same walls where film builds fastest in the lower corners of the frames,
so a house with one problem nearly always has the other.

## Two methods, chosen per window

**Reachable glass** gets the traditional treatment: a detergent solution, a
scrubber to break the film, a squeegee to pull it off in one pass. There is no
substitute for physical contact when the problem is bonded film, and on accessible
glass it is still the fastest route to a genuinely clear pane.

**Upper storeys and anything above an obstruction** get a water-fed pole running
purified water. Strip the minerals out and water dries leaving nothing behind, so
the glass can sheet dry untouched. It also means no ladder in your planting bed
and no ladder feet on your decking.

We carry our own water and filtration on every job. That is partly about result
and partly about access — a vacant property between tenants, a house with the
water shut off, a job where the outside tap is behind a locked gate.

## What is included

- Exterior and interior glass on every accessible opening
- Screens removed, washed, dried and returned to the opening they came from
- Tracks and sills vacuumed and wiped — grit here is what wears weatherstripping
- Frames wiped where they are washable
- Cobwebs cleared around openings and the eaves immediately above
- A walk-round with you at the end, in daylight, before we pack up

Skylights, glass balustrades, storm windows and conservatory glazing are quoted
separately because access varies so much house to house. Post-construction glass
is a genuinely different job — mortar splash, silicone, label adhesive and paint
overspray on new glass, where doing it wrong scratches permanently.

Not included, so there are no surprises: fog between the panes of a sealed unit,
which is a failed seal rather than dirt; paint and silicone removal as part of a
standard clean; and any window we cannot reach safely, which we identify on the
quote rather than discover on the day.
Why a sealed unit fogs, and what can and cannot be done about it, is set out in
the profile of [insulated glass units](/surface-library/insulated-glass-units/).

## Where the water goes

Worth a paragraph, because it is the part nobody else quotes on.

Bellingham's stormwater chapter admits nothing but stormwater into the drainage
system, and unlike Ferndale's code there is no exemption for routine building wash
down. Pole work produces runoff. So on a Bellingham job the rinse is directed to
planting and lawn, where soil filters it, rather than to the gutter line and the
nearest inlet — and on a Lake Whatcom watershed property, where reservoir-specific
provisions apply on top, that planning is tighter again.

Full detail is on [wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Glass varies more than people expect across this city

Worth knowing before a quote, because it changes the time and occasionally the
answer.

**The wooded south end** — Edgemoor, Fairhaven, South Hill — runs to older stock:
wood sashes, some original single glazing, divided lights with real muntins, and
storm windows that come off and go back on. More openings, smaller panes, slower
work, and sashes that want care rather than speed.

**The north and east** — Cordata, Barkley, Irongate, newer Meridian — is modern
construction: large fixed picture units, low-E coated sealed glass, and fewer but
much bigger panes. Faster per opening, harder to reach, and the low-E coating
gives a faint sheen people sometimes mistake for a film that will not come off.

**The lakeside and hillside lots** add the access problem: glass over decking,
over slopes, over mature planting, and stairwell windows nobody has cleaned since
the house was built. That is pole work, and it is the reason the pole exists.

**Waterfront and near-waterfront** carries salt film on top of everything else, on
the exposed elevations.

None of this changes the price list, because there isn't one — it changes what we
are actually looking at when we walk the property, which is why the quote happens
on site.

## How is glass handled in the historic districts?

Slowly, and with the expectation that some of it is older than the people living
behind it. Bellingham has eight National Register historic districts, from
Broadway Park and Eldridge to Sehome Hill and York. The Cissna Cottages date from
1900 to 1904, and York was substantially built out by the Great Depression, so on
those streets a window can be a century-old sash with glazing putty that has
dried and cracked.

Where original glass survives it is thinner than a modern pane and less
forgiving of pressure. On those openings we use a light hand with the scrubber,
keep blades off anything with visible ripple or seed in it, and do not force a
sash that has been painted shut. Cracked panes, crumbling putty and loose
glazing points get pointed out on the walk-round. We are cleaners, not window
restorers, so the repair is yours to decide on, but at least you hear about it
from someone who has just looked at every opening up close.

## Do you clean apartment, condo and townhome glass?

Yes, and it is a growing share of what gets built here. Roughly three-quarters of
the permits Bellingham issued over the past decade were for multi-family
housing, which means a lot of three-storey townhome rows and small condo
buildings with fixed upper glass and balconies.

Those jobs run differently from a detached house:

- **Scheduling goes through a manager or an association**, and residents need
  notice before anyone appears outside a bedroom window
- **Balcony glass** is often only reachable from inside the unit, so it needs a
  resident at home or a key arrangement agreed in advance
- **Upper fixed glass** over parking, entrances and landscaping is pole work, and
  we plan the rinse so it does not run across a shared walkway
- **Ground-floor retail** under residential floors is a different job again, and
  sits with our [commercial window cleaning](/services/commercial-window-cleaning/)

## What sets the price on a Bellingham window job?

There is no rate card, because the same opening count can mean very different
work across this city. What the quote actually comes down to:

1. **Openings and pane size.** A south-end house with divided lights has many
   small panes; a Cordata build has fewer, much larger ones.
2. **Storeys and what sits below the glass.** Decks, slopes, conservatory roofs
   and planting beds all change how the glass is reached.
3. **Storm windows and old sashes**, which take longer and want more care.
4. **How long since the last clean.** A first visit on north-side glass after
   several wet seasons is restoration; the next one is maintenance.
5. **Extras** such as skylights, balustrade glass and post-construction residue.
6. **Whether it rides with other work.** Glass booked alongside roof or gutter
   work shares one setup and one trip.

## When should Bellingham windows be booked?

For most houses, twice: once as the wet season lets go and once before it
returns. With roughly 80% of the annual rain landing between October and March,
glass cleaned in the first half of October is facing six months of weather, and
shaded exterior panes will not look freshly done by February. That is not a
reason to skip the autumn visit; it is a reason to book it for what it does,
which is getting the interior clear before the condensation months.

The spring clean is the one that shows. By March or April the heaviest weather
is behind the house, the light is coming back, and glass cleaned then stays
right for months rather than weeks. If roof or gutter work is planned for the
same year, put the glass after it, never before. And if the forecast shows a hard
frost, we will move the date rather than work on frozen panes.
