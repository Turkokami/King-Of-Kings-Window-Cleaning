Pressure washing is the one exterior service Bellingham's rules name outright. The
City does not leave wash water to general principle: pressure wash water is not to go
into a storm drain, and that decides how a driveway gets cleaned here more than the
machine does.

The other thing that decides it is the weather. A city that takes most of its rain
between October and March grows a green film on every shaded slab, step and deck
board, and by midwinter some of those surfaces are genuinely dangerous to walk on.

## What does Bellingham require of pressure wash water?

That it stays out of the storm system. Bellingham Municipal Code chapter 15.42
prohibits pressure wash water from entering a storm drain; it has to go either to the
sanitary sewer or onto a vegetated area. A street inlet at the foot of a driveway is
the wrong destination, whether or not the water carries any detergent.

It helps to picture two separate networks of pipe. The sanitary sewer takes what
leaves your sinks and toilets. The storm system exists to move rainwater off roofs and
roads. Wash water from a driveway carries tyre residue, oil drips, organic sludge and
whatever was used to loosen the moss, and the City's rule is plain about which of the
two networks it belongs in, and which it does not.

## What does that look like on a Bellingham driveway?

Most of the effort goes in before the machine starts:

- We read the slope first. A drive that falls toward the street carries its rinse
  straight to the curb; one that falls toward a lawn does half the job for us.
- Any storm inlet in the path of the water is covered or bermed.
- Rinse is steered onto grass or planting beds, where soil takes it up, rather than off
  the end of the slab.
- Beds along the edges are soaked with clean water before any solution goes down, and
  rinsed once more at the end.
- Where a site offers the water nowhere acceptable to go, the job is planned around
  recovering it, or the scope changes.

None of that adds much time. It is the difference between a clean driveway and a clean
driveway that has left a problem in the gutter at the street. The code sections, cited
in full, are on [wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Why does the City hand out reclaim kits?

Because it would rather businesses comply than guess. Bellingham gives free pressure
wash water reclaim kits to businesses, which is a fairly clear signal of how the rule
is meant to be read. A commercial forecourt, a restaurant's bin area or a shop
entrance produces far dirtier water than a household drive, and it is often sitting a
few feet from a street inlet.

For commercial clients, containment is therefore part of the scope from the first
conversation rather than something bolted on at the end. If you manage a Bellingham
property and have already had questions from the City about wash water, mention it at
the quote and the plan will be built around it.

## Why will we ask when a building went up?

Because City of Bellingham guidance flags buildings put up between 1950 and 1979 as
possibly containing PCBs, and says their wash water may need licensed hazardous
disposal. Most of our pressure washing is flat ground (concrete, pavers, decks) and we
do not pressure wash building walls at all; siding is a soft-wash job, covered under
[house washing in Bellingham](/locations/bellingham/house-washing/). Commercial work in
particular, though, can involve masonry, entry steps and surfaces tied into a
structure from that era.

We are not a testing lab or a hazardous-waste contractor, and we will not act like one.
The useful thing we can do is ask before quoting and, if the answer places a building
in that window, talk through what it means for the scope instead of discovering it
halfway through.

## Which surfaces do we pressure wash?

Hard ground and decking, with the pressure set for each one:

1. Concrete driveways, parking pads and garage aprons
2. Walkways, front steps and side paths
3. Patios in concrete, pavers or flagstone
4. Decks, timber and composite alike, at low pressure
5. Retaining walls, stair runs and other hardscape
6. Bin areas, entrances and forecourts on commercial sites

Siding, roofs and anything else upright on the house are off the list. The reasoning
behind that boundary, and how each material behaves under a jet, is set out on the main
[pressure washing](/services/pressure-washing/) page.

## Why are Bellingham paths so slippery in winter?

Because they stay wet for months at a stretch. With about 36 inches of rain a year and
roughly 80% of it between October and March, a shaded concrete path here is damp for
most of the winter and grows a thin coat of algae, with moss creeping into the joints
and along the edges. Dry, it just looks discoloured. Wet, it is slick.

Trees add to it. Bellingham's street trees are mostly maples, cherries and oaks, and
their autumn leaf fall lies on sidewalks and drives in soggy mats that stain the
concrete and feed the film beneath. Front steps under a maple are often the worst
surface on the whole property.

When a budget has to be split, we suggest the steps and the path to the door ahead of
the driveway, because that is where somebody actually falls. More on that at
[slippery driveways and walkways](/services/pressure-washing/slippery-driveways-and-walkways/).

cleaned within its manufacturer's limits, at low pressure with a solution doing most of
the work, because too much force can haze or score the surface. Of the three growths,
lichen is the slowest to let go. A deck clean can sit in the same visit as
[window cleaning](/locations/bellingham/window-cleaning/), as it did for that customer.

## How is the cleaning done?

Flat slabs get a surface cleaner, a round shrouded head with jets spinning underneath.
It holds a steady height and lays down an even finish without the stripes a hand wand
leaves behind. The wand is kept for edges, steps and corners.

Growth is treated before it is blasted. A solution put down first and given time to
work lets the machine run at moderate pressure, which is kinder to the cream layer on
concrete and to the joints between pavers. Paver jobs finish with the joints checked,
since washing out the jointing sand is the main risk on that surface.

Hot water is reserved for oil, grease and commercial bin areas, where heat shifts what
pressure alone will not. A house driveway carrying nothing worse than moss and algae
rarely calls for it.

## What changes the price?

Pressure washing is priced from the site itself, on:

- The area of each surface, and how much of it sits in shade
- The material, since pavers, old brick and decking are done more gently than
  broom-finish concrete
- How heavy the growth or staining is, from a light film to years of moss
- Access for the equipment, and a water supply
- Drainage, meaning how much work it takes to keep the water out of the storm system
- Extras such as re-sanding paver joints

A drive that slopes to the street with an inlet at the bottom needs more setting up
than one that drains onto a lawn, and that is a genuine line on a Bellingham quote.

## When is the right time in Bellingham?

Autumn and spring. Clearing steps and paths in September or October means the wet
season is spent walking on clean concrete, and the leaf fall can be dealt with at the
same visit or just after it. Spring is the busier stretch, once the winter's growth
has shown itself and people want their patio back.

Freezing weather is off the calendar, because water spread on a slab below freezing
makes the hazard worse rather than better. In Bellingham's shade, cleaned hardscape
usually greens over again within one to three years, sooner on the north side of a
house and under trees.

## Apartments, condos and shared walkways

A great deal of Bellingham's newer property is shared. Around three-quarters of the
building permits issued in the city over the past decade were for multi-family
housing, and every one of those buildings has walkways, stairs and parking that
residents use without choosing or checking them. A mossy communal step is a liability
for whoever manages it.

A scheduled autumn clean of the routes people actually walk is the sensible approach,
done in one visit across the whole site, with notice to residents about moving cars.

Shared sites also tend to have more paving running toward the street and more inlets
in the car park, so the drainage plan matters more than it does on a single house. We
walk the whole site with the manager before quoting, mark which inlets need covering
and where the rinse can go, and schedule around bin collection and the busiest parking
hours so the work does not end up competing with residents for the same few spaces.
