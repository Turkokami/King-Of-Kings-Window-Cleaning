Most houses in Anacortes do not need a wash all over at the same rate. The
shaded wall goes green, the lower courses pick up splash and grime, the side
looking over the water dulls, and the rest of the house stays more or less
presentable. A good wash starts by working out which walls are which.

Then comes the question every Anacortes wash has to answer: where does the
water go?

## What does a house wash take off an Anacortes home?

Usually four things, in different places on the same house:

- **Green and black organic growth** on north walls and anywhere shaded by
  trees, fences or neighbouring buildings
- **Grime and splash** on the bottom few courses, from rain bouncing off soil,
  paths and decks
- **A dull film** on walls facing open water or the prevailing weather
- **Cobwebs, insect nests and debris** under eaves, soffits and porch ceilings

The city sits between Burrows Bay, Guemes Channel and Fidalgo Bay, with more
than 27 miles of shoreline, so plenty of houses have at least one wall facing
open water. We have not seen any measured salt or wind data for Anacortes and
will not claim a figure. We simply look at each wall on the walk-round and
price the wash for what is actually on it.

## Does the water-facing side need a different approach?

Sometimes. The side of the house facing the water often carries a film that
looks more like weathering than growth, and it does not respond to rinsing
alone. It usually needs the solution to sit a little longer before the rinse.

Shaded walls are the opposite problem. The growth there is organic, often
visible as green or black streaks, and it clears well with the right solution
and dwell time. Treating both sides identically either underdoes the exposed
wall or overdoes the shaded one. The page on
[north-facing wall mildew](/services/house-washing/north-facing-wall-mildew/)
explains why shaded walls grow faster and what keeps them clean longer.

## What is included in a house wash?

A standard wash covers:

- **Walk-round first**, agreeing which walls, trim and features are included
- **Protection** of planting, outdoor lights, electrical fittings and vents
- **Siding** from the foundation to the eaves on every side included
- **Soffits and fascia** rinsed along with the walls
- **Trim, doors and garage doors** washed along with the walls
- **Runoff control** set up before any solution goes on
- **A final rinse** of the walls, glass and planting
- **A last walk-round** with you to check the result

Decks, fences, driveways and paths are separate jobs, priced on their own. Our
overall [house washing](/services/house-washing/) page covers the service
across the whole route.

## How does soft washing actually work?

The cleaning is done by the solution rather than by force. We apply a cleaning
solution at low pressure, starting from the bottom so it does not streak down
dry siding. We give it time to work on the growth and grime, then rinse from the
top down with gentle, even coverage.

High-pressure washing on siding causes more problems than it solves. It can
force water behind the cladding, strip paint, gouge wood, crack older vinyl and
blow out window seals. On vinyl in particular, what looks like dirt is often
oxidation of the surface itself, which aggressive washing makes worse. The
[vinyl siding](/surface-library/vinyl-siding/) profile explains how that
surface ages and what it can take.

Dry weather matters. The solution needs to stay on the wall long enough to
work, and walls need to dry afterwards. We watch the forecast and move a wash
rather than rush it into a wet week.

## Do older Anacortes homes need anything different?

Often. About 1,155 housing units in Anacortes, roughly 13% of the stock, were
built in 1939 or earlier, while the median year built is 1989. The city keeps
its own Register of Historic Places, which lists houses such as the
Wasserman-Lowman House on 8th Street and the Judd House on 9th Street.

Older wood-clad houses call for a lighter hand. Old paint can be chalky or
loose, boards can be weathered, and original windows may not seal as tightly as
modern ones. We use less water around frames and lower pressure throughout, and
we point out failing paint before we start, since a wash will lift loose paint
rather than disguise it. We do not advise on what a historic house may or may
not do to its exterior; if yours is on the register, that question belongs to
the city.

## What does Anacortes code say about wash water?

Two things, both in Anacortes Municipal Code 18.30.050.

First, it bars anyone from discharging into the stormwater system anything
other than stormwater. Its list of illicit discharges includes soap, detergent,
household and commercial cleaning materials and steam-cleaning waste.

Second, it conditionally permits routine external building wash-down that does
not use detergents, along with street and sidewalk wash water.

In 2022, Ordinance 4023 amended chapter 18.30 to set up a Source Control Program
under the city's Phase II municipal stormwater permit.

For a house wash, the upshot is plain. A rinse with water alone can fall inside
the exemption. A soft wash uses a cleaning solution, and its runoff is not
covered, so it has to stay out of the storm drains.

### How do we keep solution out of the bay?

By planning where the water goes before any of it is used. Anacortes drainage
flows to Guemes Channel, Burrows Bay, Fidalgo Bay, Padilla Bay and Similk Bay,
and a street drain is the shortest route from your siding to any of them.

On the day:

1. We find every drain inlet the runoff could reach, on the property and at the
   curb.
2. Those inlets get covered or bermed before any solution goes on.
3. Downspouts discharging toward a hard surface are redirected onto soil.
4. Runoff is steered onto lawn, beds and soil, where the ground filters it.
5. Where the site gives the water nowhere safe to go, we recover it rather than
   release it.

The general approach, with the reasoning behind it, is on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## What time of year suits a house wash in Anacortes?

Late spring through early autumn, in dry weather. On the Western Regional
Climate Center's long Anacortes record, which runs to 2013, November and
December were the wettest months, and the annual mean was 26.70 inches, less
than the mainland station near Mount Vernon. That gives a reasonable season for
work that needs walls to dry, but we still plan around the week's forecast
rather than the average.

A wash in spring clears the winter's growth before it spreads. A wash in late
summer leaves the house clean going into the wet months.

## What drives the cost of a house wash here?

The price is set on site. The main factors:

1. **Size of the house**, in wall area rather than floor area.
2. **Height.** A two- or three-storey wall on a sloping lot takes longer than a
   single-storey on level ground.
3. **Condition.** Heavy growth or years of build-up takes more dwell time and
   more care.
4. **Cladding type**, since older wood and painted surfaces need slower work.
5. **Runoff control needed**, which depends on how close the house sits to
   drains and hard surfaces.

## Should the roof be cleaned before the walls?

If the roof needs work, yes. A roof clean sends moss, grit and dirty rinse down
over the gutters and onto the siding, so washing the walls first means washing
some of them twice. The same applies to gutters: a trough that overflows in the
first heavy rain after a wash will streak the wall you just paid to clean.

On a combined visit the order is roof, then gutters, then walls, then glass.
That also means one round of garden protection and one runoff plan covering
everything, rather than setting both up again on a second day. The roof side of
that sequence, including why shingles are soft washed rather than pressure
washed, is covered on the
[Anacortes roof cleaning page](/locations/anacortes/roof-cleaning/).

If the roof is in good shape and only the walls need attention, a wash on its
own is fine. We will tell you on the walk-round which of the two you are
looking at, and we will not add a roof clean to a quote simply because we are
already there.

## Can only some walls be washed?

Yes, and on many Anacortes houses it makes sense. The north wall and the side
facing the water often need washing every year, while the sunny, sheltered
sides can go two or three. Washing only the walls that need it keeps the cost
down without letting the worst side get away from you.

We will suggest a split like that where it fits, and say so plainly where the
whole house is due. The [Anacortes overview](/locations/anacortes/) sets out the
wider conditions on the island that decide which walls those are.

## What about the driveway and paths?

A house wash stops at the foundation. Green or black growth on concrete paths,
steps and drives is a separate job, and a slippery one in winter. The page on
[slippery driveways and walkways](/services/pressure-washing/slippery-driveways-and-walkways/)
covers it. If you are having the glass done too, book it after the wash; the
[Anacortes window page](/locations/anacortes/window-cleaning/) explains the
order.
