Gutters on Fidalgo Island do not all fill at the same rate. A house on open
ground by the water can go a year between clean-outs with nothing more than
windblown dust in the trough. A house backing onto woodland can fill its
gutters in a single autumn and overflow by Christmas.

Most of the work in Anacortes is working out which of those two you have, and
setting the visits to match.

## Why do Anacortes gutters fill the way they do?

Trees, mainly. The Anacortes Community Forest Lands cover more than 2,950 acres
inside the city, with over 50 miles of trail running through them, and homes
near the edges of that ground live with its leaf fall. Away from the forest,
plenty of private lots carry mature trees of their own.

What comes off those trees ends up in the trough: leaves, needles, cones, twigs,
seed pods and fine organic matter that breaks down into a dark silt. Leaves are
the obvious problem, but they are not the worst one. Needles and silt pack
together at the bottom of the gutter and at the downspout outlet, forming a mat
that lets water pool behind it. That is why a gutter can look clear from the
lawn and still overflow in the first heavy rain.

Moss on the roof adds to the load. As it dries and loosens, clumps wash down
into the gutters and lodge at the outlets. On a house with a mossy roof and
trees overhead, the gutters are doing double duty.

## How do you tell your gutters need clearing?

Usually from the ground, if you know what to look for. The signs that a trough
is full or an outlet is blocked:

- Water spilling over the front edge in steady rain rather than only in a
  cloudburst
- A downspout that stays dry, or barely trickles, while the gutter above it
  is running
- Plants, grass or seedlings visible along the gutter line
- Dark streaks down the fascia board or the outside face of the gutter
- Splash marks, mud or worn soil along the foundation below a run
- A run that sags between hangers, often because wet debris has added weight

Any one of those is worth a look before the wet months. Two or three together
usually mean the gutter has been full for a while, and the fascia behind it
has been getting wet each time it overflows.

## When does the rain actually test Anacortes gutters?

In November and December. The Western Regional Climate Center's Anacortes
station, whose record runs from 1892 to 2013, put November at 3.84 inches and
December at 3.80 as the wettest months, against an annual mean of 26.70 inches.
That annual total is lower than the 32.58 inches recorded at a station
west-northwest of Mount Vernon, but a gutter does not care about the yearly
sum. It cares about how much arrives in a single wet week, and the late-autumn
months are when those weeks stack up.

A gutter full of wet litter going into those months is the one that spills
over the front of the trough, soaks the fascia, splashes mud up the siding and
dumps water at the foundation instead of carrying it to the downspout. The
right moment to clear it is after most of the leaf fall and before the heavy
rain starts, which in most years means October.

## What does an Anacortes gutter visit include?

A standard clean covers:

- **Every run cleared by hand**, trough and corners, with the debris bagged
- **Downspout outlets** opened up where the packed silt collects
- **Each downspout flushed** with plain water and checked for flow at the
  bottom
- **Roof edge debris** picked off where it sits ready to wash back in
- **Spillage cleaned up** from paths, decks and planting below
- **A note of anything wrong**, such as sagging runs, loose hangers, split
  seams or leaking joints

Debris leaves with us unless you want it for compost. Our general
[gutter cleaning](/services/gutter-cleaning/) page sets out the service across
the whole territory.

## How are the gutters actually cleared?

By hand, from a ladder or from the roof edge where that is safer, working the
run section by section. Wet material is scooped rather than blown. Blowing
throws debris over the roof, the walls and the windows, and wet litter from a
wooded Anacortes lot does not blow cleanly in any case.

Once the trough is empty we run water through it. That shows whether the run
falls toward the outlet the way it should, or whether water pools in a low
section. Then each downspout is flushed and we watch the bottom to confirm it
flows. A downspout blocked partway down is cleared from the top or the bottom,
whichever reaches the blockage. If water backs up where a downspout enters a
buried pipe, we tell you, since clearing an underground line is outside a
gutter clean.

## Does a wooded lot need a different schedule from an open one?

Yes, and it is the main reason two quotes on the same street can differ. A
house with no trees within reach of the roof mostly collects grit, roof granules
and whatever the wind brings, and a single autumn visit keeps it running.

A house beside wooded ground, or with its own large trees leaning over the
roof, gets a steady supply of litter through most of the year, not only in
autumn. For that house, one clean in October and a second in late winter or
spring is often what keeps the outlets open. Some very heavily treed lots need
more. We would rather tell you that on the walk-round than sell you a single
visit and leave you with an overflowing gutter in January.

## Do older houses need anything different?

Sometimes. Anacortes has a real mix of ages: about 1,155 housing units, around
13% of the stock, were built in 1939 or earlier, even though the median home
dates from 1989. Older houses can have gutters that have been replaced in
pieces over the years, with mixed profiles, tired hangers and joints that have
been patched more than once.

We take more care on runs like that. Ladders go against the wall or on
standoffs rather than on a weak gutter lip, and we keep weight off sections
that feel loose. Anything that looks like it is failing goes on the walk-round
list. We are not gutter fitters, so the repair itself is for someone who is.

## What drives the cost of gutter cleaning here?

The quote is given on site. What moves it:

1. **Length of the runs**, which sets the baseline.
2. **Height.** A single-storey ranch is quick. A two- or three-storey house on
   a hillside plot takes longer and needs more setting up.
3. **How much is in the trough.** A gutter cleared last autumn is a quick job.
   One that has not been touched in several years under trees can hold
   compacted silt with plants growing in it.
4. **Roof pitch and access**, which decide whether we work from ladders or from
   the roof.
5. **Downspout count** and whether any are blocked.

A lot under trees costs more to maintain than one in the open, and the honest
way to keep that cost down is regular visits so no single clean is a
restoration job.

## Where does the water and debris go?

Anacortes Municipal Code 18.30.050 bars anything other than stormwater from
entering the city's stormwater system. The town's drainage empties to five
water bodies: Guemes Channel, Burrows Bay, Fidalgo Bay, Padilla Bay and Similk
Bay.

Gutter cleaning fits inside that easily, as long as it is done tidily. The
flush uses plain water, sent down the downspouts the same way rain goes.
Debris is bagged rather than washed along a driveway toward a street inlet,
which is the easiest way for a gutter clean to put organic muck into a drain.
Where a job adds a cleaning solution, as with brightening the outside of the
gutters, that runoff is kept on the property. Our approach in full is on
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## What if my gutters overflow or look stained?

Overflow in steady rain usually means a blocked outlet or a packed trough,
occasionally a run that has lost its fall. The common causes and fixes are
covered on [overflowing gutters](/services/gutter-cleaning/overflowing-gutters/).
If the trough is clear and it still overflows at one spot, the problem is often
the hanger spacing or a sag, which we will point out.

The outside face of a gutter is a different issue. Black streaks down the front
come from dirty water spilling over the lip and from organic growth, and they
do not come off with a hose. That is a separate treatment, described on
[gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/).

## Should the roof be done at the same time?

If the roof has moss, yes. Soft washing and hand-lifting moss sends material
down into the gutters, so the logical order is roof first and gutters second,
on the same visit. Doing the gutters alone under a mossy roof means the first
winter storms wash loose moss straight back into a trough you just paid to
empty. The roof side of the job is explained on the
[Anacortes roof cleaning page](/locations/anacortes/roof-cleaning/).
