On a Sudden Valley house the dirt is mostly alive. The green film on the shaded
wall, the black specks under the eaves and the moss creeping along the lowest
course of siding are all growth, and they come off easily with the right
chemistry and very little force.

The harder question is where the water goes once they are off. In Sudden Valley
the answer has to be somewhere other than a storm drain, because the drains lead
toward Lake Whatcom and the lake is where the community's taps draw from.

## What builds up on Sudden Valley siding?

Mostly biology, sorted by how much sun each wall gets. The walls that stay damp
longest after rain collect the most.

- **Green algae film**, heaviest on the shaded elevations and on walls under or
  near trees.
- **Mildew**, showing as black or grey speckling under the eaves, around
  windows and behind shrubs planted close to the house.
- **Moss and lichen** on the lowest courses, where splash keeps the siding wet.
- **Overflow streaks** running down from a gutter that has been spilling over.
- **Cobwebs, wasp nests and general grime** in the soffits and around the
  entry.
- **Chalking** on older painted or vinyl siding, which is sun damage rather than
  dirt.

The pattern of which wall goes green first is predictable once you know what to
look for, and it is explained on the page about
[north-facing wall mildew](/services/house-washing/north-facing-wall-mildew/).
In Sudden Valley the shaded wall is often not the north one at all, but whichever
side the trees or the slope keep out of the sun.

## Why is wash water the central question in Sudden Valley?

Because the county says so, and because of what the drains lead to. Whatcom
County's guidance on pressure washing, which cites WCC 16.36.050, tells residents
to collect all wash water and keep it out of storm drains and waterways. Sudden
Valley is unincorporated, so this is the rule that governs, and the community is
also one of the named areas inside the county's NPDES Phase II municipal
stormwater permit area.

The reason it matters so much here is the lake. The Lake Whatcom Water and Sewer
District draws Sudden Valley's supply from Basin 3 of Lake Whatcom. Rinse water
off a house wash carries dead algae, grime and traces of cleaning solution, and
if it runs down a driveway to a grate, the next stop is the lake the neighbours
drink from.

A house wash produces more water than any other job we do on a home. That makes
it the job where containment planning does the most good.

## Why soft wash rather than pressure wash?

Because siding is built to shed water running down it, not water fired at it.
Every common cladding, whether vinyl, fibre cement, painted wood lap or cedar
shingle, relies on overlapping courses that keep gravity-fed water on the
outside. A pressure washer drives water sideways and upward into those overlaps
and around window flashings, into a wall that may then stay damp for weeks.

It also damages the surface directly. Brittle old vinyl cracks; the
[vinyl siding profile](/surface-library/vinyl-siding/) explains why age makes the
difference. Paint lifts off wood. Cedar furs and splinters.

Soft washing does the same job gently. A solution chosen for the growth and the
siding goes on at roughly garden-hose pressure, sits long enough to kill what is
living there, and is rinsed off with the same low pressure. The growth comes away
with the rinse instead of being blasted into the wall.

## What kind of siding will we find on a Sudden Valley house?

Anything, which is why we look before we mix a thing. The community was built
in two big waves: 993 of its homes date from the 1970s and 844 from 2000 to 2009,
with only 105 built since 2010. A fifty-year-old house may still carry its
original cladding under several coats of paint, or may have been re-sided along
the way. A twenty-year-old house is usually on its first siding, and the
question is keeping it clean rather than rescuing it.

The difference changes the solution strength, the dwell time and how gently the
rinse needs to be. Painted surfaces with failing paint, in particular, need a
light touch, and we will point out anything flaking before we start rather than
after.

## What does a house wash include?

The standard wash covers the whole exterior skin of the house:

- All siding on every elevation, from the foundation line up to the eaves.
- Soffits and the underside of the eaves, including cobwebs and nests.
- Fascia boards and the outside faces of the gutters, where they can be reached
  safely.
- Exterior trim around doors and windows.
- Entry doors and garage doors, washed gently.
- Pre-soaking and post-rinsing of planting next to the house.
- A walk-round with you at the end.

Decks, driveways and paths are separate jobs with separate equipment. Algae on
concrete is a slip hazard as much as a cosmetic one, which is covered under
[slippery driveways and walkways](/services/pressure-washing/slippery-driveways-and-walkways/).

## Where does the rinse water actually go?

Somewhere we have chosen in advance. On a Sudden Valley property the plan runs
like this:

1. **Read the lot** to see which way the ground falls and where the nearest drain
   inlets are.
2. **Cover any inlets** within reach of the work area before a drop of solution
   is mixed.
3. **Soak the planting** along the foundation with clean water so it takes up
   very little of what follows.
4. **Wash in sections**, applying only what each wall needs rather than flooding
   the whole house.
5. **Steer the rinse** onto lawn, beds and soil, and away from driveways and paths
   that run to the road.
6. **Rinse the planting again** once each section is finished.

The same thinking, set out with the codes behind it, is on our
[wash water and storm drains page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Is a house wash only about appearance?

No, though the appearance is what most people notice first. Growth on siding
holds moisture against the surface, and a wall that never fully dries is a wall
whose paint fails sooner, whose caulking breaks down faster and whose wood trim
starts to soften at the joints. Taking the growth off lets the surface dry
between showers again, which is the condition every exterior material was
designed for.

There is a practical side too. A washed wall shows its problems. Peeling paint,
cracked panels, gaps around a window frame and soft trim all hide under a layer
of green, and they are much easier to spot, and cheaper to fix, once the wall is
clean. We point out anything we notice; the repair itself is a job for a painter
or a carpenter.

### How long does a washed house stay clean in Sudden Valley?

It depends almost entirely on shade. A sunny, open wall can stay clean for a
couple of years. A wall under trees, facing a bank or tucked behind tall shrubs
will start to show a green tint again sooner, sometimes within a season or two.
Trimming shrubs back from the foundation and keeping the gutters above clear
both help the wash last, and neither needs a permit.

## What affects the price of a house wash here?

Every quote comes from a visit, and these are the factors that shift it:

1. **Size of the house**, measured in wall area more than floor area.
2. **Height.** A two-storey wall above a slope takes more setup than a
   single-storey one over lawn.
3. **Siding type and condition**, because fragile or painted surfaces are slower.
4. **How much growth is there.** A light film washes quickly; years of moss on
   the lower courses do not.
5. **Containment work**, such as drains to cover and planting to protect.
6. **Add-ons** like gutter faces, decks or the garage.

## When should a Sudden Valley house be washed?

Any mild, dry stretch outside a hard frost. The solution needs to stay wet on the
wall long enough to work, which is harder in hot, direct sun and pointless in a
heavy downpour. Late spring and early autumn usually give the most workable days.

Many owners also book a wash before selling or before a repaint. A painter
needs clean, dry siding to work on, and a wash a week or two ahead of the
painter saves them doing a rushed job of it themselves.

## What usually follows a house wash?

Clean glass. Washing the walls inevitably puts some splash on the windows, so
the sensible order is walls first, then glass on the same day; the
[Sudden Valley window cleaning page](/locations/sudden-valley/window-cleaning/)
covers that half of the job. If the overflow streaks on the siding came from a
blocked gutter, the gutter needs clearing too, or the streaks will be back by
spring.
