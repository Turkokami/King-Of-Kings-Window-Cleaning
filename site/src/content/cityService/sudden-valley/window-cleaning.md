Windows in Sudden Valley pick up a particular mix. Tree debris and pollen land
on the glass, rain running off a mossy roof leaves streaks down the panes, and
shaded frames stay damp long enough for green growth to set up in the corners.
Where a house faces the lake or the fairway, those windows are usually the ones the
family actually looks through all day.

Glass is also the last thing we clean on a house, so it is the job where
everything else we have done, or not done, shows.

## What makes Sudden Valley windows dirty?

A handful of local habits, most of them tied to shade and trees:

- **Tree debris and pollen** that settle on sills and wash down the glass in the
  next shower.
- **Roof runoff streaks**, where rain carrying grit and dead growth off the roof
  spills over a gutter and down the panes.
- **Green growth in the frame corners** and along the bottom rail, where water
  sits longest on a shaded window.
- **Cobwebs and insect debris** around the openings and under the eaves, which
  build up fast on a house close to trees.
- **Interior film** from cooking, heating and condensation through the wet
  months.

None of that is exotic. It simply arrives faster on a house surrounded by trees
than on one in open ground, and the cleaning schedule has to allow for it.

## What glass will we find on a Sudden Valley house?

Two generations of it, broadly speaking, because the community was built in two
waves. Census estimates date 993 of Sudden Valley's 2,985 housing units to the
1970s and 844 to the years 2000 through 2009, with only 105 added since 2010.

A first-wave house has had fifty years to have its windows replaced, and many
owners will have done so at least once, but some openings may still hold older
units with aluminium or wood frames, tired weatherstripping and tracks that have
seen a lot of grit. Those need a careful hand on the tracks and the sashes.

A house from the 2000s wave usually has double-glazed units that are now roughly
twenty years old. That is the age at which some sealed units begin to fail,
which shows as fog or a milky film trapped between the panes. It cannot be
cleaned off because it is inside the sealed space. The
[insulated glass units profile](/surface-library/insulated-glass-units/) explains
what is happening and what the options are. We point out any units showing it
during the walk-round, so you are not left wondering whether the clean worked.

## What does a window clean include?

The standard visit in Sudden Valley covers:

- **Exterior and interior glass** on every opening that can be reached safely.
- **Screens** taken out, washed, dried and returned to the same opening.
- **Tracks and sills** vacuumed and wiped, since grit left there grinds at the
  weatherstripping every time the window slides.
- **Frames** wiped where the material allows.
- **Cobwebs** cleared from around the openings and the eaves just above them.
- **A final look** at the glass with you, in daylight.

Skylights, glass railings, storm windows and sunroom glass are priced separately
because access varies so much. Solar panels are a different job again, with its
own approach, covered on the [solar panel cleaning page](/services/solar-panel-cleaning/).

## How is the glass actually cleaned?

Two methods, and the choice is made window by window.

Glass within reach from the ground or a short ladder gets a traditional clean. A
scrubber loaded with a mild detergent solution works over the whole pane to break
up film, and a squeegee pulls the water off in a controlled pass, with the edges
finished by hand.

Upper-storey glass, and anything over a deck, a bank or a garden bed, gets a
water-fed pole running purified water. The water has had its dissolved minerals
removed, so it dries on the glass without leaving marks, and the pane can be left
to dry on its own. That lets us clean a second-storey window from the ground
without leaning a ladder on your decking or your plants.

We carry our own water and filtration. No verified hardness figure is available
for the Lake Whatcom Water and Sewer District's supply, so rather than guess
what is in the outside tap, we bring water we know is clean. It also means we can
work on a vacant house or one with the outside water turned off.

## Will marks and spots come off the glass?

Most of them, and we will tell you on the quote which ones will not. It helps to
separate three kinds of mark.

Loose dirt, pollen and cobweb sit on the surface and come off in any clean.
Film is dirt that has been wetted and dried onto the pane, often from roof
runoff or overflow, and it needs detergent and the scrubber rather than a quick
rinse. Mineral spotting, from sprinklers or water drying on the glass, is a
deposit rather than dirt, and fresh spotting lifts with a treatment made for it.

The exception is glass that has been spotted for years. Old deposits can etch
the surface of the pane, and an etch is damage to the glass itself; no cleaning
removes it. We check for it before we start, so the outcome is agreed in advance
rather than discovered at the end.

## Does window cleaning have a runoff angle here?

A small one, but yes. The traditional method uses very little water, and most of
it stays on the scrubber and the towel. The pole method uses more, though what
runs down the wall is purified water carrying the dust and film that was on the
glass.

Even so, a Sudden Valley job is planned with the drains in mind. Whatcom County's
guidance on washing, which cites WCC 16.36.050, asks that wash water be kept out
of storm drains and waterways, and the storm drains here lead toward Lake
Whatcom, the source of the community's tap water. Buckets of used detergent
solution are emptied onto soil or taken with us, never tipped into a street
grate. The [wash water and storm drains page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/)
explains our approach in more detail.

## What decides the price of window cleaning in Sudden Valley?

The quote comes from a look at the house, and it rests on these:

1. **Number of openings**, the starting point.
2. **Size of the panes.** A handful of big view windows can take longer than a
   dozen small ones.
3. **Height and access**, especially glass above decks, banks and planting.
4. **Condition.** Glass cleaned last season is quick; glass carrying years of
   film, streaks and frame growth is not.
5. **Screens, tracks and extras** such as skylights or railing glass.
6. **Inside as well as out**, which roughly doubles the glass surface.

The second clean on a house almost always comes in lower than the first, because
it is upkeep rather than a rescue.

## When should windows be cleaned in Sudden Valley?

Spring and early autumn, for most homes. Spring clears what the wet months left
behind, and early autumn gives you clean glass for the darker half of the year,
when you are indoors looking out.

Timing also depends on what else is happening on the house. If the roof is being
treated, the gutters cleared or the walls washed, the windows should come last,
because every one of those jobs puts water and debris on the glass. The
[Sudden Valley house washing page](/locations/sudden-valley/house-washing/)
explains that part of the sequence.

A hard frost is the one reason we move a date. Water freezing on the glass or in
the tracks is no good for the window or the result.

## How long does a typical visit take?

Longer than the window count alone would suggest, for most Sudden Valley houses.
A single-storey home with open access and standard windows is often a morning's
work, inside and out. A two-storey home with big view panes, glass over a deck
and screens on every opening can take most of a day. First visits run longer
than later ones, because the tracks, frames and screens are usually carrying
more than a season's grime. We give you a realistic time on the quote, so you
know whether to plan around us for a morning or a whole day.

## What about views of the lake and the golf course?

They are the reason some Sudden Valley windows get more attention than others.
The community sits on Lake Whatcom and includes a marina and an 18-hole golf
course designed by Ted Robinson, and homes facing those tend to have the glass
the owners care most about. If you have a favourite window, tell us at the start.
We take extra time on it and check it with you at the end, from inside, where
the view actually is.
