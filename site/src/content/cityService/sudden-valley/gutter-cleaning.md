A Sudden Valley gutter works for its living. Many homes here sit under trees
that drop needles, twigs, cones and seed onto the roof through every season,
and the whole lot slides down into a trough that was sized for rain, not for a
small forest floor.

What happens to that material once it leaves the gutter matters here in a way it
does not in most towns. Storm drains in Sudden Valley lead toward Lake Whatcom,
and the lake is where the community's drinking water comes from.

## Why do Sudden Valley gutters fill up so fast?

Because the trees stay. A lot of homes in the community live under a real
canopy, and in the Lake Whatcom watershed Whatcom County requires a Tree Removal
Permit before any tree on the lot comes down, unless the removal is part of an
approved development. The option that people in other places reach for first,
cutting the offending tree back to nothing, is off the table for most owners
here, or at least not something to do on a whim.

That leaves the gutters doing the work. Where the trees are conifers, needles are
the main culprit. They fall
in small amounts all year, knit together into a wet mat along the bottom of the
trough, and trap silt blown or washed off the shingles. After a season or two
the mat becomes a thin layer of soil, and gutters that hold soil start growing
moss and, now and then, a seedling. From the lawn, a gutter in that state still
looks empty, because the mat lies below the lip.

## What goes wrong when a gutter overflows here?

The same list as anywhere, with one more line at the bottom. Water that cannot
reach the downspout spills over the front edge and lands at the base of the wall.
Fascia boards stay wet and start to soften. Siding under the overflow streaks and
grows algae. The ground against the foundation saturates and stays that way
through the wet months. We go through the warning signs on the
[overflowing gutters page](/services/gutter-cleaning/overflowing-gutters/).

The extra line in [Sudden Valley](/locations/sudden-valley/) is where that water goes next. Overflow from a
clogged gutter does not fall clean; it carries silt, rotting needles and loose
granules with it, and on a sloping lot a good part of it heads for the road and
the drains. The county is building stormwater projects inside Sudden Valley to
cut the phosphorus reaching the lake. A gutter that dumps organic
sludge onto the driveway every storm is working, in a small way, against that.

## What does a gutter clean include?

Each visit covers the whole system, not just the troughs:

- **Every gutter run cleared by hand**, including the inside corners and the
  ends behind the downspout outlets, where material piles up first.
- **All debris bagged** at the gutter and taken off the property.
- **Downspouts flushed with clean water** to confirm they carry water freely
  from top to bottom.
- **Blockages lower in the pipe cleared** by hand or rod, not blasted onward.
- **Roof valleys and the lower edge of the roof** swept of loose debris that
  would otherwise wash straight back into a clean gutter.
- **A look at the hangers and the slope** of each run, with anything sagging or
  pulling loose pointed out to you.
- **A walk-round at the end**, so you see the finished gutters and hear what we
  noticed.

What is not included: repairs, new hangers, re-sloping a run, or installing
guards. If the gutter needs a carpenter or a gutter installer, you will hear that
from us rather than finding out later.

## How is the debris handled on a watershed lot?

As something to remove, never something to relocate. The routine on a Sudden
Valley property is simple and it does not change job to job.

Debris comes out of the gutter by hand and goes into a bag held at the gutter,
so very little of it reaches the ground. Anything that does fall is picked up.
Nothing is tipped into the trees at the back of the lot, raked into a drainage
ditch or left in a pile beside the driveway for the next rain to carry away.

Downspout flushing uses plain water and only after the trough is clear, so the
water running through the pipe is mostly clean. Where a downspout feeds a
buried drain line, we are careful not to drive a plug of sludge down into it.
That plug has to come out at the top.

Whatcom County's washing guidance, which cites WCC 16.36.050, asks that wash water
be collected and kept out of storm drains and waterways. Gutter work produces
very little wash water compared with a house wash, but the principle is the same,
and the fuller explanation lives on the
[wash water and storm drains page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## Which Sudden Valley houses take the most time?

The ones with long rooflines, steep pitches and trees right over the eaves. Age
plays a part too. About a third of the community's homes date from the 1970s, and
a fifty-year-old house may be on its second or third set of gutters, or still on
an old set that has settled out of level; either way the runs need looking at
rather than assuming. A home from the 2000s building wave is younger, but every
extra inside corner and valley in its roofline is another place for debris to
collect, and those take time to clear properly.

Access matters as much as the house itself. A gutter over a deck, a steep bank
or a garden bed takes more setup than one over a flat lawn, and the quote
reflects that.

## What affects the cost of gutter cleaning here?

We price on site, and five things decide the number:

1. **Total length of gutter**, which sets the baseline.
2. **Height and pitch.** A single-storey eave is quicker than a two-storey one
   over a slope.
3. **How full the gutters are.** A system cleaned last year clears fast. One
   holding three years of packed needles, with moss growing in it, takes far
   longer.
4. **Access around the house**, including decks, banks, planting and parked
   vehicles.
5. **Add-ons** such as clearing the roof valleys more thoroughly or cleaning the
   outside faces of the gutters.

A second visit on a regular schedule almost always costs less than the first,
because we are keeping the system clear rather than digging it out.

## When should gutters be cleaned in Sudden Valley?

Before the wet season, and again after the heaviest drop, is the pattern that
suits a treed lot. Late spring clears what came down over winter and gets the
downspouts running before the dry months. Autumn, once most of the season's
debris has fallen, is the visit that matters most, because that is the system
you will be relying on through the rain. Very shaded houses sometimes need a
third visit before midwinter.

A house with open sky above it can usually get by on one clean a year. We will
tell you honestly which kind of house you have.

The worst time to discover a blocked downspout is during the first long spell of
autumn rain, with water already sheeting over the front of the gutter. Booking
the autumn visit a little early, and adding a quick top-up later if the trees
are still dropping, beats waiting for the overflow to announce itself.

## Does county law touch gutter work in the watershed?

Mostly indirectly, and it is worth knowing where. Sudden Valley is one of the
named areas inside Whatcom County's NPDES Phase II municipal stormwater permit
area, which is why the county treats what goes down its drains as its business.

Two watershed rules come up in conversation after a gutter clean. The first is
the tree permit already described. The second is timing: ground-disturbing work
in the Lake Whatcom watershed is limited to June 1 through September 30. If we
find a downspout that empties against the foundation and you decide to run a new
drain line away from the house, that digging falls inside the window, so plan
the repair for summer rather than for the week after your autumn clean.

As for the community association, we have not been able to read its rules and
make no claim about them. If you are considering new gutters or guards after
our visit, check with the association before ordering anything.

## What other problems show up alongside clogged gutters?

A few, and they tend to travel together on a shaded Sudden Valley house:

- **Roof moss.** Moss on the shingles sheds into the gutter as it grows and dies,
  so a mossy roof refills a clean gutter quickly. The
  [roof cleaning page for Sudden Valley](/locations/sudden-valley/roof-cleaning/)
  covers that side of the problem.
- **Tiger striping.** Dark vertical streaks on the outside face of the gutter
  come from water and grime running over the lip. They clean off separately; see
  [gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/).
- **Stained siding under the overflow**, which usually comes back until the
  gutter above it is kept clear.
