A roof in Sudden Valley has two jobs that pull against each other. It has to
shed rain from a house that often sits in shade, which is the exact setting
moss and algae prefer. And everything that rinses off it, whether rain or a
cleaning crew's water, is on its way toward Lake Whatcom.

Cleaning a roof here means solving the first problem without adding to the
second. That shapes the method, the timing and the order of work on every Sudden
Valley roof we take on.

## What grows on Sudden Valley roofs, and why?

Moss, algae and lichen, in roughly that order of how often we see them on a
shaded roof. Shade keeps a roof damp for hours or days after rain, and that damp
is what they all need.

- **Moss** settles first in the grooves between shingles and on the north side of
  the ridge, then spreads into cushions that hold water against the roof and
  lift the edges of the courses.
- **Algae** shows as dark vertical streaks running down from the ridge. On
  composition shingle it feeds partly on the filler in the shingle and spreads
  with the water flow.
- **Lichen** forms flat grey or pale-green rosettes that grip the surface hard
  and are slow to release even once they are dead.

A good many homes in the community sit under canopy, and on a watershed lot most
of those trees are staying. Whatcom County requires a Tree Removal Permit to
remove any tree in the Lake Whatcom watershed unless it is part of an approved
development. So the shade that grows the moss is, for most owners, part of the
house, and the realistic plan is to treat the roof on a sensible interval rather
than to remove the cause.

## Why does the lake matter most on a roof job?

Because the roof is the largest surface on the house and it is designed to send
every drop of water in one direction: into the gutters, down the downspouts and
away. On a roof-cleaning day that efficiency works against you, since whatever
the rinse carries off the shingles gets delivered quickly to wherever the
downspouts discharge.

Lake Whatcom has been on Washington's 303(d) list of polluted waters since 1998,
when it failed the state standard for dissolved oxygen, and it is under a cleanup
plan for phosphorus and bacteria. The lake is also the drinking water source for
more than 120,000 people. Whatcom County names the Sudden Valley Community
Association as the largest development in the watershed and is building
stormwater projects there to cut the phosphorus that reaches the lake.

Dead moss and algae are organic matter. Roof grit carries whatever has settled on
the roof. Cleaning solution, however carefully applied, is something the lake
does not need. None of that should reach a storm drain, and on a roof the
drainage system is built in, so the plan has to start at the downspouts.

## How is a Sudden Valley roof cleaned?

By soft washing, which swaps pressure for chemistry and patience. The solution
is chosen for the roofing material, applied at roughly garden-hose pressure,
given time to kill what is growing, and rinsed gently. Nobody walks the roof with
a pressure wand, and nobody scrapes it.

The material changes the details:

- **Composition shingle** tolerates a standard soft-wash approach, but its
  granules are its weather protection and must not be blasted off. The
  [composition asphalt shingle profile](/surface-library/composition-asphalt-shingle/)
  explains why.
- **Cedar shake and shingle** needs a gentler solution and more care still,
  because the wood fibres lift and fur under force. See the
  [cedar shake roof profile](/surface-library/cedar-shake-roof/).
- **Metal roofing** is treated on its own terms, with attention to the coating
  and the seams.

What you see on the day is not the finished result. Algae streaking usually
lifts right away. Thick moss dies, goes brown and loosens over the next few weeks
of weather. A roof that looks fully clean an hour after the truck leaves has
usually been scraped or pressure washed, and it has lost something doing it.

## How do we keep roof runoff away from the drains?

With a routine set up before the solution comes out, and the steps are the same
on every Sudden Valley roof:

1. **Walk the lot first** to see where each downspout discharges and where the
   ground falls, because a driveway that runs to the road is a different problem
   from a lawn that holds water.
2. **Deal with the downspouts** that empty into a buried line or onto paving,
   redirecting them onto soil or recovering the rinse where the job allows.
3. **Cover drain inlets** within reach of the work area.
4. **Soak planting below the roof** with clean water before anything is applied,
   and rinse it again afterwards.
5. **Apply only what the roof needs**, working in sections rather than flooding
   the whole slope at once.
6. **Bag the loose moss and debris** that comes down, rather than washing it off
   the edge.

Whatcom County's washing guidance cites WCC 16.36.050 and asks that wash water be
collected and kept out of storm drains and waterways. Our fuller reasoning is on
the [wash water and storm drains page](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/).

## What does a roof clean include?

A standard roof visit covers:

- A look at the roof and gutters from the ground and the ladder before any work
  starts, with anything that looks like damage pointed out to you.
- Loose moss and debris cleared from valleys and the roof edge by hand.
- Soft wash treatment of the whole roof surface for moss, algae and lichen.
- A gentle rinse where the material and the conditions call for it.
- Protection and rinsing of planting, paths and siding below.
- A clear explanation of what the roof will look like over the following weeks.

Gutters fill with dead moss as a treated roof clears, so most owners book the
[Sudden Valley gutter cleaning](/locations/sudden-valley/gutter-cleaning/) for the
same day or a few weeks afterwards.

## What drives the cost of roof cleaning here?

The number comes from a visit, and these are the things that move it:

1. **Roof area**, which sets how much solution and time the job takes.
2. **Pitch and height.** A steep two-storey roof needs more setup and more care.
3. **Material.** Cedar is slower and more careful work than composition.
4. **Amount of growth.** Heavy moss cushions take more clearing and treatment than
   a light green film.
5. **Runoff control.** Downspouts to redirect, drains to cover and planting to
   protect all add setup time on a watershed lot.
6. **Access** around decks, banks and planting beds.

A roof kept on a regular interval costs less per visit than one left until the
moss is lifting shingles.

## When is the right time to clean a roof in Sudden Valley?

During a dry spell, whatever the month. The solution needs time on the roof to
work, and a heavy shower too soon afterwards washes it off before it has done its
job, sending it into the gutters with nothing gained. We watch the forecast and
move the date rather than treat a roof with rain on the way.

Spring and early autumn tend to give the most usable weather. Spring treatment
means the moss is dead and loosening through the drier months. Early autumn
treatment knocks growth back before the long wet season. A hard frost is a reason
to wait, because a wet roof at freezing is no place for a technician or a
solution.

## How long does a treated roof stay clean here?

Longer in sun, shorter in shade, and the trees decide most of it. A roof with
open sky above it dries quickly after each shower, and growth takes a long time
to come back. A roof under canopy that the owner cannot or will not thin stays
damp, and spores find it again sooner. There is no honest single number for the
whole community, which is why we would rather look at your roof and your trees
and give you an interval for that house.

Two habits stretch the gap between treatments. Keeping the gutters and valleys
clear stops debris from holding water on the lower courses, where regrowth
usually starts. And catching early green film with a light treatment is far
easier on the roof, and on the budget, than waiting for thick cushions to form
again.

## What related problems should Sudden Valley owners watch for?

Moss that has been growing for years does more than look untidy. Its cushions
lift shingle edges and let water work underneath, and the damage is often hidden
until the moss is gone. The page on
[moss under shingles](/services/moss-removal/moss-under-shingles/) sets out what
to look for. We will show you anything we notice, but we are cleaners, not roof
inspectors, and a lifted or cracked shingle is a roofer's call.

Siding under a mossy roof often grows its own green film, and dirty glass below
an untreated roof streaks every time it rains, so a roof clean is often the first
job in a sequence rather than the only one.
