An Everson roof spends most of the year damp, and a damp roof grows things. Moss
takes hold along the lower edge and in the shade of trees, black algae streaks
down from the ridge, and lichen settles into little crusty patches that grip
tighter every year.

None of that is a failure of the roof. It is what happens to exposed surfaces in
a wet valley. The job is to clear it without damaging what the roof is there to
do, which is keep water out.

## Why do Everson roofs grow so much moss?

Moisture, shade and time. The WRCC's Clearbrook cooperative station is a regional
record rather than one in Everson itself, but it gives a fair picture of the
wetness around here: an average of 46.71 inches of precipitation a year, with
November and December each above six inches.

A roof in those conditions is wet for weeks at a stretch through the dark
months. Moss spores land, find grit and moisture in the joints between shingles,
and root in. North-facing slopes, sections under trees and the shade of a taller
neighbour stay wet the longest and grow the most.

Everson sits in the Nooksack River valley, about 15 miles northeast of Bellingham, and a roof there dries on the valley's schedule, not the calendar's. The growth pattern shows it: the sunny side of a ridge can look almost clean while the
shaded side carries a thick green mat.

## What is actually growing up there?

Three things, and they need slightly different handling.

- **Moss** is the soft green growth that soaks up and holds rain. It
  collects in the gaps and along the eaves, and left alone it lifts shingle
  edges and feeds debris into the gutters.
- **Algae** shows as dark streaks, usually running downslope. It is mostly
  cosmetic but holds moisture and makes a roof look older than it is.
- **Lichen** forms flat, pale, crusty patches. It anchors firmly into the
  surface and is the slowest of the three to release.

When moss has worked its way under the edges of the courses, the problem is
harder to see and more important to deal with. Our page on
[moss under shingles](/services/moss-removal/moss-under-shingles/) explains
what that looks like and why it matters.

## How old are Everson roofs, and why does it matter?

Older than people often assume. The median Everson home was built in 1987, and
the town's two biggest building decades were the 1970s and the 1980s, with 237
and 219 units respectively out of 1,074.

A house from those decades may be wearing its original roof, or a replacement
fitted somewhere along the way. Either way, a lot of the roofing in town is past
its first flush of youth. An older composition shingle has already shed some of
its granules, the mineral layer on top that shields the asphalt from sun. It
cannot afford to lose more to a heavy-handed clean.

That is the practical reason our method is gentle by design. On a roof that has
some years behind it, the difference between a soft wash and a pressure wash is
the difference between a cleaner roof and a shorter-lived one.

## How do we clean a roof without damaging it?

With a soft wash: low pressure, a treatment suited to the roofing material, and
time for it to work.

1. **Protect the ground first.** We pre-wet planting under the eaves and move
   or cover anything sensitive.
2. **Clear loose debris** from valleys and the roof edge by hand or soft brush.
3. **Apply the treatment at low pressure**, roughly garden-hose force, working
   from the bottom up so it is not running onto dry sections.
4. **Let it dwell** so it kills the moss, algae and lichen down to the roots.
5. **Rinse the planting and the walls** beneath so nothing is left to dry on
   them.

Algae streaks usually brighten the same day. Moss dies and browns, then
loosens and washes off with the rain over the following weeks, so the roof
keeps looking better after we have gone. Anyone who removes every trace of thick
moss on the day is scraping or blasting it, and both cost the roof life. For
more on why we avoid pressure, see the main
[roof cleaning](/services/roof-cleaning/) page.

## What drives the cost of an Everson roof clean?

Every roof is quoted on site. The price depends on:

- **Roof area and pitch**, since steep roofs need more setup and care
- **Height**, with two-storey eaves taking longer than a single-storey ranch
- **Roofing material**, as cedar and older shingle take more time
- **Amount and type of growth**, with thick moss and lichen being the slowest
- **Access around the house**, including trees, fences and outbuildings
- **Whether gutters are cleaned on the same visit**, which is usually worth it

A roof that has been kept on a regular cycle is quicker, and cheaper, to clean
than one with a decade of moss. The first treatment does the heavy lifting.

## What time of year suits an Everson roof clean?

Spring through early autumn, in a spell of dry weather. The treatment needs a
few dry hours to work, and it should not be washed off by rain before it has
done its job.

Late spring is popular, because moss has done most of its growing over winter
and the roof has time to clear before the wet season returns. Early autumn is
the other good window, especially if you want to pair a roof clean with a
gutter clean before November. We do not treat roofs in freezing conditions or
when heavy rain is forecast within the day.

## Where does the runoff go?

Down the gutters and downspouts, like any rain, and then into lawn and planting
where possible. We manage the amount of treatment we use, rinse the plants and
the ground beneath, and avoid sending anything toward a street drain.

## What else should be done with a roof clean?

The gutters, most of the time. Once the treatment has killed the moss, the
debris washes down into the channel over the following weeks. If the gutters are
already partly full, that extra load is often what tips them into overflowing.
Booking an [Everson gutter clean](/locations/everson/gutter-cleaning/) for a few
weeks after the roof, or at your usual autumn visit, keeps the system flowing.

We will also mention anything we notice that is outside our job: a lifted flashing,
a cracked vent boot, missing shingles. We are not roofers and do not inspect roofs
for structural problems, but we would rather tell you what we saw than keep quiet.

## How can you keep a roof cleaner for longer?

A clean roof in a wet valley will eventually re-green; the aim is to push that
day further out. A few habits make a real difference, and none of them cost
much:

- **Let the light in.** Trimming branches that overhang the roof is the single
  biggest thing you can do, because shade is what keeps a slope wet.
- **Keep the roof clear of debris.** Needles and leaves piled in a valley or
  behind a chimney hold moisture and give moss a seedbed.
- **Keep the gutters flowing**, so water is not backing up under the bottom
  course of shingles.
- **Look up after big storms**, especially in November and December, for
  branches or debris that have landed and stayed.
- **Do not wait for a thick mat.** Light regrowth treated early comes off more
  easily and with less risk to the shingle than a roof left for many years.

What we would steer you away from is climbing up with a stiff broom or a garden
pressure washer to deal with the first patches yourself. It is a fall risk, and
scrubbing a wet composition roof loosens the granules that are protecting it.
If you notice regrowth and are not sure whether it needs attention yet, send us
a photo from the ground and we will give you an honest view on timing.
