A gutter in the Nooksack valley has one serious job and two months to prove it
can do it. Everything that falls on the roof between spring and autumn sits in
the channel waiting for the first proper storm, and in Everson that storm is
rarely far off once October ends.

This page covers how we clean gutters here, what we find in them, and why the
last step of the job, checking where the water goes after it leaves the
downspout, matters more on valley ground than it does up on a hillside.

## Why do Everson gutters work hardest in November and December?

Because that is when the rain arrives in bulk. We do not have a gauge inside
Everson to quote, so we lean on the WRCC's Clearbrook cooperative station, a
regional record from elsewhere in the county. It averages 46.71 inches of
precipitation a year, and November and December each average more than six
inches.

Those two months are when a half-full gutter stops being a cosmetic problem.
A run that coped with September showers will overtop in a December downpour,
and it tends to happen at night, in wind, when nobody is outside watching it. The
first sign is often a stripe of dirt down the siding or a puddle against the
foundation the next morning.

Timing a clean just ahead of that stretch is the single most useful thing an
Everson homeowner can do for their gutters.

## What actually ends up in an Everson gutter?

More variety than people expect, because this is a farm town with trees rather
than a forest town with houses. On a typical valley property we pull out:

- **Leaves and seed pods** from yard and street trees, heaviest in October
- **Conifer needles**, which mat into a dense layer and plug downspout outlets
- **Shingle granules**, the grit that composition roofs shed as they age
- **Moss** that has broken off the roof edge and started growing in the channel
- **Fine dust and soil** blown in from worked fields and dried into a sludge
- **The occasional ball, toy or nest**, which we set aside rather than bin

The granules and the fine soil are the ones that catch people out. They are
heavy, they settle at the bottom of the channel under the leaves, and they turn
into a wet paste that holds water long after the surface looks clear. A gutter
that looks empty from the lawn can still be carrying a centimetre or two of it.

## Why does a low, wet lot make overflow worse?

Because saturated ground has nowhere to put the extra water. The Nooksack is
closely watched here; the National Weather Service keeps a gauge called
"Nooksack River Overflow At SR544 Everson", and its flood-impact statement warns
that overflow water crosses Main Street and SR 544.

You do not have to live in the flood zone for the valley's wetness to matter.
Low ground near a river holds water through the winter, and a gutter that dumps
its load at the base of a wall is adding to soil that is already full. That is
how crawlspaces stay damp, how splash stains creep up the lower siding, and how
a patio slab starts to settle at one corner.

So on every Everson job we look at the outlet end as carefully as the channel:
where each downspout discharges, whether the splash block is still in place,
and whether the water is running away from the house or pooling beside it.

Where a downspout feeds an underground drain line rather than a splash block, we
flush it and watch for water backing up at the joint. A buried line that has
filled with silt and roots will push water straight back out of the elbow, and
from the ground that looks exactly like a gutter problem. We cannot camera or
dig out a buried pipe, but we can tell you which downspouts are affected so the
right trade gets called for the right fault.

## What does the job include?

The standard clean on an Everson house covers:

1. **Hand-clearing every run**, front and back, including porch and garage roofs
2. **Bagging and removing the debris** rather than dropping it in your beds
3. **Flushing each downspout** and confirming it runs clear at ground level
4. **Clearing outlets and elbows** where a plug has formed
5. **A quick look at hangers, seams and slope**, reported to you in plain terms
6. **Picking up anything that fell** during the work before we pack up

Brightening the outside face of the gutters is a separate job. If yours have the
dark vertical tiger striping that aluminium picks up over time, ask about
[gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/) on the same
visit.

## How do we clean gutters without damaging anything?

By hand, from ladders set up so their weight never rests on the gutter itself. A gutter is a thin sheet of aluminium or vinyl hung from the
fascia, and it bends far more easily than people realise. Leaning a ladder
straight onto the front edge is how runs end up with dents that hold water
permanently.

We scoop the debris out, then rinse the channel with a low-flow hose toward the
outlet so we can see the water move. If it stalls, we find out why. A blocked
downspout gets flushed from the top, and if that does not shift it we work from
the bottom as well.

On single-storey homes and most manufactured homes the work is done entirely
from ladders. Two-storey houses and roofs with a lower section below an upper
one may need us to step onto the roof, which we only do where the pitch and
surface allow it safely. Where a moss-covered roof edge is shedding into the
gutter, we will point it out, because cleaning the channel under an untreated
roof just buys you a few months.

## What drives the cost on an Everson house?

We do not publish prices for Everson; we quote once we have seen the building.
The factors that move the number are consistent, though:

- **Total length of gutter**, counted in runs rather than square footage
- **Height and storeys**, since two-storey eaves need taller ladders and more setup
- **Building type**: detached houses make up about 70% of Everson's homes, but the
  town also has 110 units in three- and four-unit buildings and 75 mobile homes,
  and each has its own layout
- **How long since the last clean**, because packed sludge takes longer than loose leaves
- **Guards or screens**, which have to be lifted, cleared under and refitted
- **Add-ons** such as brightening, or pairing the visit with a roof clean

The usual pattern is that the first clean costs the most and regular cleans
after it cost less, because we are maintaining rather than excavating.

## How often should gutters be cleaned in Everson?

Once a year for an open lot with few trees nearby. Twice a year, autumn and
spring, for most homes with mature trees in the yard or next door. Properties
right under heavy conifer cover can need more, and we will tell you honestly
after the first visit whether yours is one of them.

Our advice for timing in this valley is simple: aim to have the channels empty
before November. A late-October clean catches the bulk of the leaf drop and
leaves the system clear for the two heaviest months. A spring visit then deals
with winter needles, catkins and any moss that has crept in.

## Where does the rinse water and debris go?

Somewhere sensible, and never down a street drain. We bag the solids and take
them away. The small amount of rinse water from flushing the downspouts is plain
water, and it runs out through your own downspouts into the same place your
rain goes.

We could not verify Everson's own stormwater code when this page was written,
so we make no claim about what it requires. Rules vary from one jurisdiction to
the next across Whatcom County, and our
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/) page
explains how we handle runoff in general and cites the codes we have read. One
detail about Everson's setting does shape our habits: the city's backup wells
draw from a shallow, unconfined sand-and-gravel aquifer, and that is reason
enough to keep anything other than clean water off the ground.

## What problems does a blocked gutter cause here?

Water in the wrong place, mostly. The common knock-on effects we see on valley
homes are:

- Overflow streaks and splash staining on the lower siding
- Soft or rotting fascia boards behind a run that has held water for years
- Green film on the wall below a leaking seam or corner
- Damp crawlspaces and puddling against the foundation
