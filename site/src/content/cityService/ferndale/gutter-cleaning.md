A gutter in Ferndale has a simple job and a demanding setting. It catches the
water coming off the roof and moves it to a few chosen points on the ground. Here,
a lot of those points sit on flat ground not far from a river that went over its
banks in 2021, so a gutter that spills its load at the wrong spot
does more harm than it would on a hillside.

This page covers what we find in Ferndale gutters, how we clear them, and what
decides the cost and the timing.

## Why do Ferndale gutters need their own plan?

Because the town is young, and young houses get their gutters ignored.

The census puts the median year built for Ferndale housing at 1998, and 1,690 of
the town's roughly 6,200 homes went up in 2010 or later. On a house like that the
gutters are usually sound, correctly pitched and well fixed. What they often are
not is checked. The owner has never needed to look, the gutter looks fine from the
lawn, and the first sign of trouble is water sheeting over the front edge in a
November downpour.

Three other things feed into it:

- **The river.** Local news reported the Nooksack flooding parts of Ferndale on
  16 November 2021, including Main Street near the Pioneer Bridge and the I-5
  on-ramp at Main Street. We do not claim to know how any given lot drains. We do
  treat each downspout's discharge point as part of the job, because roof water
  dropped against a wall on damp ground has the fewest places to go.
- **The roof above.** Moss and lichen shed debris into the trough, and shingle
  granules wash down with every rain. Both settle as grit at the bottom of the
  gutter and in the downspout outlet.
- **Mixed housing.** The same street can carry a two-storey family home, an older
  single-storey house and a manufactured home, and each has different gutters,
  different heights and different weak points.

## What does a Ferndale gutter visit include?

Every visit covers the same core work, whatever the house:

1. **Every gutter run cleared by hand,** front and back, with the debris bagged as
   we go.
2. **Debris hauled off the property,** not tipped into the beds or left on the
   lawn.
3. **Each outlet checked and cleared** where the downspout leaves the gutter, which
   is where most plugs form.
4. **Every downspout flushed and flow-tested** with water, so you know it drains
   rather than assuming it does.
5. **The discharge point looked at,** because a downspout that empties against the
   foundation is doing half its job.
6. **A short condition note** on anything we noticed: loose hangers, leaking
   seams, sections holding standing water, soft fascia, missing splash blocks.
7. **Roof edge, walls and paths rinsed** of anything dislodged during the work.

Skylights, very tall walls and roofs we cannot safely reach are identified on the
quote rather than on the day. The outside face of the gutter is a different job,
covered further down.

## How is the work done?

From a ladder, in most cases, placed so that no weight goes on the gutter itself,
and moved along the run as we work. On a single-storey house with a
walkable, low-pitch roof we may clear from the roof edge instead, but only where
the roofing can take foot traffic without damage.

Clearing by hand is slower than blowing and it is the right call here. A blower
moves dry, loose leaves. It does not move a wet layer of grit and granules packed
into the bottom of the trough, and whatever it does move ends up on your roof,
your windows and your planting. Clearing by hand also puts a pair of eyes on the
whole length of the gutter, which is how loose hangers and failing seams get
found.

Once the trough is clear we put water in at the far end of each run and watch it
travel. It should reach the outlet without pooling and leave at the bottom of the
downspout at the same rate it went in. If it does not, the blockage is somewhere
between, usually at the first elbow, and we clear it from whichever end reaches it.

Where a downspout disappears into a buried pipe and the water backs up at ground
level, the fault is in the underground line. That is drainage work rather than
gutter cleaning, and we will say so instead of pretending a gutter visit fixed it.

## What do we usually find on a newer Ferndale house?

Less debris than on an old house and more surprises. The common findings on a
first visit to a house under twenty years old:

- **Shingle granules** in a layer along the bottom of the trough. New composition
  roofs shed loose granules freely in their first years, and they collect at the
  outlets.
- **Construction leftovers** such as offcuts, fasteners and sealant scraps that
  landed in the gutter during the build and have sat there ever since.
- **Outlets without strainers,** or strainers that have clogged and are now
  holding water back rather than keeping debris out.
- **Downspouts discharging too close to the wall,** often because a splash block
  shifted or an extension was never fitted.

None of these is serious on the day we find it. Left for a few more winters, each
one can become a stained fascia, a wet crawlspace or a gutter pulling away from
its fixings.

## What about manufactured homes and apartment buildings?

Ferndale has 411 mobile homes by the census count, and they are a different
exercise. Gutters on a manufactured home are often lighter-gauge, the roof is
usually shallow, and the eaves can be close to the ground. The work is gentler and
the ladder placement more careful, but the steps are the same: clear it by hand,
flush it, test it.

Apartment buildings are quoted separately. Height, access and working around
residents' parking all change the job, and a building of twenty or more units is
not priced like a house.

## What drives the cost of gutter cleaning in Ferndale?

The quote is given on site, after we have looked at the roof and the gutters, and
it comes down to a handful of things:

- **Linear footage,** which is the starting point.
- **Height.** A second storey, a steep yard or a walk-out basement adds ladder
  work and time.
- **Access.** Fences, planting beds, conservatories and decks under the gutter
  line make the ladder harder to place safely.
- **How long since it was last done.** A trough cleared last year is a quick
  visit. One holding several years of compacted grit is not.
- **Downspout problems.** A plug in an elbow is usually part of the visit. A
  blocked underground line is not something a gutter clean resolves.
- **Extras,** such as gutter brightening or pairing the visit with a roof clean.

Pairing gutters with a roof soft wash is the most common combination, and the
order matters: roof first, gutters after, so whatever comes off the roof leaves
through clean gutters rather than into clogged ones.

## When should Ferndale gutters be cleared?

Late autumn, once the leaves around the property have come down and before the
long wet stretch settles in. That timing catches the year's heaviest debris and
leaves the gutters clear for the months when they carry the most water.

A second visit in spring suits houses with trees overhanging the roof, and it
also clears whatever the winter storms left behind. A house on open ground with no
trees nearby can often manage with one visit a year, and we will say so if that is
the case.

The time to call outside the schedule is when you see water coming over the front
edge in steady rain, a downspout that stays dry while the gutter spills, or a
gutter run that has started to sag. Each of those is visible from the ground, and
each gets worse with every storm.

## Where does the water go, and what do the rules say?

Gutter cleaning is mostly dry work. The debris is bagged and removed, and none of
it gets washed into a drain. The water we use is plain water, run through the
downspouts to test them, following the same path rain would.

Inside Ferndale city limits, Municipal Code 13.35.020(B)(4) lists "routine external
building wash down that does not use detergents" as an allowable discharge, which
covers a plain rinse of the roof edge and walls after a clean.

The outside-face job is different. Gutter brightening uses a cleaner to lift the
grey streaking, so its rinse falls outside that allowance and is contained and
directed to vegetation instead. In the unincorporated part of the Ferndale Urban
Growth Area, the county's illicit-discharge rules in WCC chapter 16.36 apply. The
full comparison, with citations, is on our
[wash water and storm drains](/compliance/wash-water-and-storm-drains/) page.

## Which problems usually start in the gutter?

More than most people connect to it. A few that we see follow directly from a
gutter that is not doing its job:

- **Overflow.** Water sheeting over the front edge in heavy rain, usually because
  an outlet is plugged. The causes and fixes are set out on the page about
  [overflowing gutters](/services/gutter-cleaning/overflowing-gutters/).
- **Tiger striping.** Dark vertical streaks on the outside face of the gutter,
  which is what [gutter brightening](/services/gutter-cleaning/gutter-brightening/)
  removes.
- **Streaked siding and splash-stained lower courses,** from water coming over
  the edge rather than down the spout.
- **Moss on the lowest rows of shingles,** where a full gutter keeps the roof edge
  wet. That is a roof question, covered on
  [roof cleaning in Ferndale](/locations/ferndale/roof-cleaning/).
