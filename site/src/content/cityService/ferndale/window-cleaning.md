Ferndale is not Bellingham with a different postcode, and the glass shows it. Ten
miles north, on the flat ground where the Nooksack finishes its run to the bay,
the air carries a different load. There is an industrial corridor to the west.
There is working farmland on three sides. There is a river that keeps the low
ground damp most of the year. All three land on your windows, and they land in a
particular order through the year.

That is the whole argument for cleaning windows here on a Ferndale schedule
rather than a generic one.

## Why Ferndale windows get dirty differently

Three things drive it, and they do not arrive at the same time.

**The Cherry Point corridor sits west of town.** Prevailing weather here comes off
the water and moves inland, which puts a large industrial area upwind of most of
Ferndale for a good part of the year. The residue this leaves is fine, even, and
persistent — it is not the sort of visible splash you can point at. It reads as a
haze that dulls the glass, most obvious when low winter sun comes in at an angle
and the whole window looks slightly grey rather than dirty. What is actually out
there, what we can honestly say about it and what we will not claim is set out in
[Cherry Point industrial fallout](/surface-library/cherry-point-industrial-fallout/).

**The valley throws dust in late summer.** Fieldwork through August and September
puts an enormous amount of very fine agricultural dust into the air. It settles
on everything, and on glass it combines with whatever moisture is around
overnight to form a film that dries on rather than rinsing off. This is the
single most reliable seasonal pattern in Ferndale window cleaning: glass that
looked fine in July looks tired by the middle of September, every year, without
fail.

**Mineral spotting is older than the water now coming out of the tap.** The
City softens its well water and has run full reverse osmosis since July 2025 —
it first added RO in 2014 specifically to bring the hardness down. But glass
remembers: years of sprinkler overspray from before that, or from a property
still on its own supply, dried on the pane and left the mineral behind. So does rain running off a mineral-stained sill.
Over a few seasons this builds a spotting pattern that ordinary washing does not
touch, because the problem is no longer dirt sitting on the glass — it is
deposit bonded to it.

Add the humidity that comes with living near a floodplain, which keeps
north-facing glass damp long enough for organic growth to take hold in the
corners of the frames, and you have a set of conditions that do not match what a
crew coming up from further south would expect.

## What is included

The standard residential clean covers:

- **Exterior and interior glass** on every accessible opening
- **Screens removed, washed, dried and replaced** in the opening they came from
- **Tracks and sills vacuumed and wiped** — grit here is what wears out the
  weatherstripping
- **Frames wiped down** where they are washable
- **Cobweb removal** around the openings and the eaves immediately above them
- **A walk-round at the end** with you, in daylight, before we pack up

Skylights, glass balustrades, storm windows and conservatory glass are quoted
separately because the access varies so much house to house. So is
post-construction glass, which is a genuinely different job — see below.

What is *not* included, so there are no surprises: we do not do interior
condensation between double-glazed panes, because that is a failed seal and no
external cleaning reaches it. We do not remove paint or silicone from glass as
part of a standard clean. And we do not clean windows we cannot reach safely,
which we will identify on the quote rather than discover on the day.

## How the work is actually done

Two methods, chosen per window rather than per house.

**Ground-floor and reachable glass** gets the traditional treatment — a
detergent solution, a scrubber to break the film, and a squeegee to pull it off
in a single pass. There is no substitute for physical contact when the problem is
bonded film, and this is still the fastest way to a genuinely clear pane on
accessible glass.

**Upper storeys, and anything above an obstruction**, get a water-fed pole
running purified water. Purified water is the point, not the pole: water with the
minerals stripped out dries without leaving anything behind, so the glass can be
left to sheet dry with no squeegee contact at all. It is the only sensible way to
handle a second-storey window over a deck, a conservatory roof, or a planting bed
you would rather nobody set a ladder in.

We carry our own water and filtration, so what dries on your glass is water
with nothing left in it — whatever your own supply is. On a property with its own
source rather than city water, that difference is the whole point: letting
mineral-bearing water dry on glass is exactly how the spotting problem starts.

Hard-water spotting, where it is present and treatable, is a separate stage
before the general clean, using a treatment made for mineral deposit rather than
for dirt.

## What window cleaning costs in Ferndale

Pricing is per property, quoted on site, and it comes down to five things:

1. **Opening count**, which is the base — but only the base.
2. **Pane size.** A newer Ferndale build with a handful of large picture windows
   can take longer than an older farmhouse with thirty small ones.
3. **Access.** Ground-floor glass on open lawn is quick. Second-storey glass over
   a deck, a slope or mature planting is not.
4. **Condition on arrival.** Glass cleaned last season is a maintenance visit.
   Glass with three years of field dust and mineral spotting is a restoration
   visit, and it costs accordingly the first time and less every time after.
5. **Extras** — screens, tracks, skylights, balustrade glass, storm windows.

Two things worth knowing before you compare quotes. First, the second clean on a
property almost always costs less than the first, because we are maintaining
rather than restoring. Second, a quote given over the phone without anyone
looking at the house is a guess, and in a town where the condition of the glass
varies this much street to street, it is usually a guess in somebody's favour.

## When to book, through the Ferndale year

**Late winter into spring.** The most-booked window of the year, and rightly so.
Winter leaves a combination of rain splash, organic film in the frame corners and
interior condensation residue. Getting it off in March or April means you spend
the light half of the year looking through clean glass.

**Early autumn, after the fieldwork.** This is the Ferndale-specific one. Book
before the dust and you will be booking again. Waiting until the harvest dust has
settled — generally late September into October — means the clean lasts through
the winter rather than being undone in a fortnight.

**Ahead of listing a house.** Clean glass changes how a room photographs more
than almost anything else you can do for the money, because it changes the light
in the room rather than the contents of it.

**After construction.** New builds and remodels leave the worst glass we see:
mortar splash, silicone smears, adhesive from factory labels, and paint overspray
on glass that is often brand new and still under warranty. It is a specialist
clean, and doing it wrong scratches glass permanently, which is why it is quoted
separately and why we would rather do it than have somebody try it with a razor
blade and a bottle of spray.

The one time we will suggest waiting is a hard frost. Cleaning glass at or below
freezing is bad for the glass and bad for the result.
